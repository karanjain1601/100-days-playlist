Two phenomena that break the classical story

Double descent

Classical statistics predicts a U-shaped test error as model capacity grows: underfit, sweet spot, overfit. What large networks actually show is that test error rises to a peak at the interpolation threshold — the point where the model has just enough parameters to fit the training set exactly — and then falls again as you keep growing. Past that threshold, more parameters make things better. This is why "bigger model, more overfitting" is bad advice in the modern regime, and why the field kept scaling long past the point classical theory said to stop. The same shape appears along the epoch axis too, which is why a val loss that turns up at epoch 40 may turn back down by epoch 200.

Grokking

On small algorithmic tasks, a network will reach 100% training accuracy while validation accuracy sits at chance — and then, tens of thousands of steps later, with training loss already flat, validation accuracy suddenly jumps to 100%. The model memorised first and generalised much later, once weight decay had ground away the memorising solution and left the structured one. The practical lesson is narrow but real: a flat training loss does not mean nothing is happening. Watch weight norms and validation, not just training loss, before killing a run.

Before you believe any curve

Establish the baselines first. What does random guessing score? What does predicting the majority class score? What does logistic regression on raw features score? What would a competent human score? A validation accuracy of 92% means nothing until you know that the majority class is 91%. Most of the time this takes ten minutes and reframes the entire project.
