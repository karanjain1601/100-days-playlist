The shift

A discriminative model estimates \(p(y\mid\mathbf x)\). A generative model estimates \(p(\mathbf x)\) — or at least gives you a way to sample from it. That is a strictly harder problem: to generate a convincing photograph you must implicitly know about geometry, lighting, materials and object structure, none of which anyone labelled.

The trilemma

FamilySample qualityDiversity / coverageSampling speed
AutoregressiveHighHighSlow — one element at a time
VAEBlurryHighOne forward pass
GANVery highMode collapseOne forward pass
FlowsModerateHighFast
DiffusionVery highHighSlow — but see Lesson 108

For years the received wisdom was that you could pick two of the three. Diffusion took quality and diversity, accepted slow sampling, and then attacked that weakness with distillation and few-step methods — which is a useful strategic lesson: take the two properties that are hard to fix, and engineer your way out of the one that is merely expensive.

Why VAEs are blurry and GANs are not

It follows from the loss. A VAE minimises reconstruction error, typically MSE, which as Lesson 21 showed corresponds to a Gaussian likelihood — and the optimal prediction under MSE when the model is uncertain is the mean of the plausible outputs. Average several plausible faces and you get a blur. A GAN's loss says nothing about matching any particular target; it says "make the discriminator unable to tell". A blurry image is trivially detectable, so blur is punished directly. Different loss, different failure mode, and the difference is entirely explained by Lesson 8's maximum-likelihood framing.

Pitfall · evaluating generative models

There is no good single metric. FID compares the mean and covariance of Inception features between real and generated sets, and it is the standard — but it is sensitive to sample count, to the resizing and JPEG settings used, and to the fact that Inception was trained on ImageNet, so it measures ImageNet-like features. Inception Score is worse: it cannot detect mode collapse within a class and it ignores the real data entirely. Report Precision/Recall for generative models (quality and coverage separately), fix your sample count and preprocessing, and state them. Two papers reporting "FID 8.3" may not be comparable at all.
