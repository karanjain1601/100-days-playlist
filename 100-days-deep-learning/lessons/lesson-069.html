Skip-gram

Given a centre word, predict its context words. Maximise, over the corpus:

\[ \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{-c\le j\le c,\ j\ne0}\log p\big(w_{t+j}\mid w_t\big), \qquad p(w_O\mid w_I)=\frac{\exp\big(\mathbf v'_{w_O}\!\cdot\mathbf v_{w_I}\big)}{\sum_{w=1}^{V}\exp\big(\mathbf v'_{w}\!\cdot\mathbf v_{w_I}\big)} \]

Note that each word has two vectors: \(\mathbf v\) when it is the centre, \(\mathbf v'\) when it is the context. The trained embedding you export is usually \(\mathbf v\), sometimes their sum.

Negative sampling — why the softmax had to go

That denominator sums over the whole vocabulary. With \(V=10^6\) and billions of training pairs, computing it once per pair is hopeless. Negative sampling replaces the multi-class problem with a set of binary ones: distinguish the true context word from \(k\) randomly drawn "negatives".

\[ \log\sigma\big(\mathbf v'_{w_O}\!\cdot\mathbf v_{w_I}\big)+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\E_{w_i\sim P_n}\Big[\log\sigma\big(-\mathbf v'_{w_i}\!\cdot\mathbf v_{w_I}\big)\Big] \]

Cost drops from \(O(V)\) to \(O(k)\) with \(k=5\)–20. The noise distribution matters: \(P_n(w)\propto U(w)^{3/4}\), the unigram frequency raised to the ¾ power. That exponent flattens the distribution so that rare words are sampled as negatives more often than their raw frequency would allow — it was found empirically and it works substantially better than either \(U(w)\) or uniform. This same contrastive shape reappears as InfoNCE in Lesson 63 and in CLIP in Lesson 109.

Subsampling handles the opposite end: words like "the" appear so often they dominate the updates while carrying little information. Each occurrence of word \(w\) is discarded with probability \(1-\sqrt{t/f(w)}\), \(t\approx10^{-5}\). This both speeds training and improves the vectors, because it effectively widens the context window past the function words.

From counts to a semantic map · co-occurrence, then SVD, then geometry

GloVe and FastText

GloVe makes the count connection explicit. Rather than sliding a window and predicting, it factorises the global co-occurrence matrix directly, fitting \(\mathbf w_i\T\tilde{\mathbf w}_j+b_i+\tilde b_j\approx\log X_{ij}\) with a weighting function that damps very frequent pairs. The insight is that the ratio of co-occurrence probabilities encodes meaning: \(P(\text{solid}\mid\text{ice})/P(\text{solid}\mid\text{steam})\) is large, \(P(\text{water}\mid\cdot)\) is near 1 for both. Differences of vectors capture ratios of probabilities — which is precisely why vector arithmetic works at all.

FastText represents a word as the sum of its character n-gram vectors, so running shares structure with runner and runs. This gives it embeddings for words never seen in training — the same OOV fix as subword tokenization, one level up — and makes it markedly better for morphologically rich languages like Finnish or Turkish.

Pitfall · the analogy result is weaker than its reputation

\(\mathbf v_{\text{king}}-\mathbf v_{\text{man}}+\mathbf v_{\text{woman}}\approx\mathbf v_{\text{queen}}\) is the most-quoted result in NLP. Two caveats that are almost always omitted. First, the standard evaluation excludes the three input words from the candidate set — without that exclusion the nearest neighbour is usually king itself, because the arithmetic barely moves the vector. Second, accuracy is high on geographic and morphological analogies and much weaker on the abstract relational ones people find impressive. The geometry is real; the demonstration is stage-managed.

Bias is not a bug in the method

Embeddings trained on human text reproduce human associations, including \(\mathbf v_{\text{man}}-\mathbf v_{\text{woman}}\approx\mathbf v_{\text{programmer}}-\mathbf v_{\text{homemaker}}\). The WEAT test quantifies this by measuring differential association between target and attribute word sets, and it recovers documented human-implicit-association results from the corpus alone. Debiasing by projecting out a "gender direction" reduces the measured score but has been shown to mostly hide the structure rather than remove it — the clusters persist and are recoverable. Treat bias measurement as diagnostic, and handle it at the decision layer of your system rather than assuming a geometric patch solved it. Lesson 118 returns to this properly.

Why static embeddings had to end

Word2Vec assigns one vector per word type. But "bank" in river bank and bank account are different words that happen to be spelled the same, and a single vector must average them — landing somewhere useless to both. Every polysemous word is a compromise, and English is full of them. Contextual embeddings, where the vector is a function of the whole sentence, are the fix; that is what ELMo started and BERT completed, and it is where Phase 6 goes.
