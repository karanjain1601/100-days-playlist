The architecture

Forward pass, written out:

\[ \mathbf h^{(1)}=\phi\!\left(W^{(1)}\mathbf x+\mathbf b^{(1)}\right),\quad \mathbf h^{(2)}=\phi\!\left(W^{(2)}\mathbf h^{(1)}+\mathbf b^{(2)}\right),\quad \mathbf z=W^{(3)}\mathbf h^{(2)}+\mathbf b^{(3)} \]

Note the last layer has no activation — logits go straight into the loss (Lesson 10).

Pitfall · why \(\phi\) cannot be the identity

Without a nonlinearity, \(W^{(3)}W^{(2)}W^{(1)}\mathbf x = W_{\text{eff}}\mathbf x\). A hundred stacked linear layers collapse into one. Depth buys you exactly nothing. The nonlinearity is not a detail — it is the only thing that makes depth mean anything.

Universal approximation: what it does and does not say

Says: a network with one hidden layer and a non-polynomial activation can approximate any continuous function on a compact set to arbitrary accuracy, given enough hidden units.

Does not say: how many units (possibly exponentially many), that gradient descent will find them, that the result will generalise, or that one hidden layer is a good idea. It is an existence proof about representation, not a statement about learning.

Depth vs width, in practice

Depth is exponentially more parameter-efficient for compositional functions. A function built from \(L\) composed pieces may need \(O(L)\) layers or \(O(2^L)\) hidden units in a shallow net. Real data is compositional — edges compose into textures compose into parts compose into objects — which is why depth wins for images, language and audio.

Practical rules for MLPs: 2–4 hidden layers covers most tabular and small-image work; widths as powers of two (128, 256, 512) for kernel efficiency; constant or gently tapering width beats an aggressive funnel. Past 4 layers a plain MLP starts to need the machinery of Phase 3 (normalisation, residuals, careful init) to train at all — which is exactly what Days 26 and 32 are about.

Hidden units as learned features

Each hidden unit is a neuron from Lesson 18, so each row of \(W^{(1)}\) is a template over the input. Layer 2's templates are over layer 1's outputs — templates of templates. Visualise \(W^{(1)}\) on MNIST after training and you will see stroke and blob detectors nobody designed. That emergence is the entire promise of the field, and you can see it on Day 24.
