High-Dimensional Statistics

Reading: HDS Ch. 6–11 · BvdG Ch. 2–9 · HDP Ch. 4 & 9. Classical asymptotics fixes p and sends n→∞. Real problems have p growing with n, and in that regime intuition from the classical picture is not merely imprecise — it is wrong about the sign of effects.

The High-Dimensional Regime

Day 37 · 85 min

What does a cloud of n points in ℝp look like when p≍n?

Three counterintuitive facts

  • Mass on the shell. For X∼N(0,Ip), ‖X‖=√p(1+Op(p−1/2)). The Gaussian is not concentrated at the origin; it lives on a thin shell of radius √p. "The mode" is statistically irrelevant in high dimension.
  • Near-orthogonality. Two independent random unit vectors have inner product Op(1/√p). Everything is almost orthogonal to everything, which is why random projections work (Lecture 58) and why ℓ1-based recovery can succeed.
  • Distance concentration. maxi‖xi‖/mini‖xi‖→1: nearest and farthest neighbours become indistinguishable, which is the precise form of the curse for k-NN (Lecture 87).
Theorem — Marchenko–Pastur

Let X be n×p with i.i.d. entries of mean 0, variance 1, and p/n→γ∈(0,1]. The empirical spectral distribution of Σˆ=1nX⊤X converges weakly to the MP law with density fγ(x)=√(λ+−x)(x−λ−)2πγxon[λ−,λ+],λ±=(1±√γ)2, plus an atom of mass 1−1/γ at 0 when γ>1.

Read the consequence: even when Σ=I exactly, the sample eigenvalues spread over [(1−√γ)2,(1+√γ)2]. At γ=0.5 that is [0.086,2.91] — a 34× spread from pure noise. Every interpretation of a scree plot must be made against this null, not against a flat line. The largest eigenvalue fluctuates on scale n−2/3 around λ+ with a Tracy–Widom limit, which is the correct null for testing "is there a signal?".

Marchenko–Pastur and the BBP phase transition
Theorem — BBP transition (spiked covariance)

Let Σ=I+ℓvv⊤. In the proportional limit, λ1(Σˆ)→{(1+√γ)2,ℓ≤√γ(1+ℓ)(1+γ/ℓ),ℓ>√γ|⟨vˆ1,v⟩|2→{0,ℓ≤√γ1−γ/ℓ21+γ/ℓ,ℓ>√γ.

Below the threshold ℓ=√γ the top sample eigenvector is asymptotically orthogonal to the truth — PCA returns pure noise while still producing a top eigenvalue that looks large. This is not an estimation-error issue that more careful computation fixes; it is an information-theoretic wall. Compare with Davis–Kahan (Lecture 7): the eigengap vanishes exactly at the transition.

Sparse Recovery and Compressed Sensing

Day 38 · 85 min

m linear measurements, p≫m unknowns, but only s of them nonzero. When is recovery exact?

ℓ0 minimization is NP-hard (reduction from exact cover). The convex relaxation is basis pursuit: min‖β‖1 s.t. Aβ=y.

Null space property — exact characterization

Basis pursuit recovers every s-sparse β iff for all v∈ker(A)∖{0} and all |S|≤s: ‖vS‖1<‖vSc‖1. Necessary and sufficient — but not checkable.

RIP — checkable in the random case

A satisfies RIP of order s with constant δs if (1−δs)‖v‖2≤‖Av‖2≤(1+δs)‖v‖2 for all s-sparse v. If δ2s<√2−1, basis pursuit recovers exactly; with noise ‖y−Aβ‖≤ϵ, the LASSO/Dantzig solution obeys ‖βˆ−β‖2≲ϵ+‖β−βs‖1√s (stable and robust: exact sparsity is not needed, only compressibility).

Which matrices satisfy RIP? Gaussian, sub-Gaussian and Bernoulli matrices do with high probability once m≳slog(p/s); partial Fourier / randomly subsampled orthonormal systems need m≳slog4p. Verifying RIP for a given matrix is NP-hard, which is why every guarantee is stated for random designs.

RIP vs RE — the honest comparison

RIP demands near-isometry on all sparse vectors, which fails for any design with correlated columns — i.e. every real covariate matrix. The restricted eigenvalue condition (Lecture 46) only requires a lower bound on the cone 𝒞(S) and is implied by, e.g., Σ≻0 with n≳slogp for sub-Gaussian rows (Raskutti–Wainwright–Yu). Statisticians should quote RE; engineers designing measurement matrices may quote RIP. Papers that assume RIP for observational data are assuming something false.

Covariance and Precision Matrix Estimation

Day 39 · 85 min

Σˆ is unbiased. Why is it useless when p≍n?

Because unbiasedness is entrywise while the operations you perform are spectral. ‖Σˆ−Σ‖op≍‖Σ‖(√p/n+p/n) (matrix Bernstein, Lecture 5), so with p≍n the operator-norm error is Θ(1) — and when p>n, Σˆ is singular, so Σˆ−1 does not exist at all.

Ledoit–Wolf is the right default. It is a James–Stein estimator for matrices: shrink the noisy eigenvalues toward their mean, with intensity estimated from the data. Its improvement is largest exactly where Σˆ is worst.

Gaussian graphical models

For X∼N(0,Σ), Θ=Σ−1 and Θjk=0⟺Xj⊥Xk∣Xrest. So estimating a graph is estimating the zero pattern of the precision matrix.

Graphical lasso

Θˆ=argmaxΘ≻0logdetΘ−tr(SΘ)−λ‖Θ‖1,off. Concave in Θ (Lecture 8). Optimality: Θ−1−S−λZ=0 with Z∈∂‖Θ‖1, i.e. |Σˆjk−Sjk|≤λ off the support — the estimated covariance matches the sample covariance exactly on the graph edges and is within λ elsewhere.

Neighbourhood selection (Meinshausen–Bühlmann) instead runs p separate lasso regressions of Xj on the rest and takes an AND/OR rule over the supports. It is consistent under an irrepresentable condition, is embarrassingly parallel, and does not return a PSD matrix — which matters if you need Θˆ downstream rather than just the graph. Latent-variable GGMs (Chandrasekaran–Parrilo–Willsky) decompose Θ=S−L with S sparse and L low-rank: unobserved common causes induce dense correlation that a purely sparse model would misattribute to many spurious edges.

Low-Rank Matrix Estimation

Day 40 · 85 min

Fill in 5% of a matrix and recover the rest exactly. Under what conditions is that not absurd?

Incoherence

M=UΣV⊤ of rank r is μ-incoherent if maxi‖U⊤ei‖2≤μr/n1 and similarly for V. Necessity: M=e1e1⊤ is rank one but invisible unless you sample entry (1,1). Incoherence says the signal is spread out, so random sampling sees it.

Theorem — exact matrix completion (Candès–Recht, Candès–Tao, Gross)

If M is μ-incoherent of rank r and m≳μr(n1+n2)log2(n1+n2) entries are observed uniformly at random, then min‖X‖* s.t. XΩ=MΩ recovers M exactly with high probability. The count r(n1+n2−r) is the degrees of freedom, so the sample complexity is optimal up to logs.

Noisy version. min12‖XΩ−MΩ‖F2+λ‖X‖* has prox = singular value soft-thresholding, giving the SVT algorithm; the error is ≲√r(n1+n2)log/m⋅σ — the nuclear norm plays exactly the role ℓ1 plays for vectors, and the analogy extends to the atomic-norm framework of Lecture 47.

Robust PCA. M=L+S with L low-rank and S sparse; principal component pursuit min‖L‖*+λ‖S‖1 with λ=1/√max(n1,n2) separates them exactly under incoherence plus a randomness assumption on S's support. Identifiability requires both — a sparse matrix that is also low-rank cannot be attributed.

Nonconvex is faster and now provably fine. Parameterize X=UV⊤ and run alternating minimization or gradient descent on the factors. Cost drops from O(n3) per SVD to O(mr) per iteration. Two guarantee styles: (i) local — spectral initialization lands in a basin, then geometric convergence; (ii) landscape — for matrix completion/sensing under RIP, all local minima are global and all saddles are strict, so perturbed gradient descent (Lecture 71) converges from a random start. Note the landscape result is about the optimization surface, not about statistical accuracy; both are needed.

Post-Selection Inference

Day 41 · 85 min

You ran the lasso, kept 8 variables, then fit OLS on those 8 and reported p-values. What is wrong?

The reported p-values answer the question "would this coefficient be significant if these 8 variables had been chosen in advance?" — a question about a counterfactual analysis nobody ran. The realized selection event conditions the data, so the null distribution is truncated, not t.

Polyhedral lemma (Lee, Sun, Sun & Taylor)

If y∼N(μ,σ2I) and the selection event is {Γy≥0}, then for any fixed η, conditional on the event and on the component of y orthogonal to η, η⊤y|{Γy≥0}∼TN(η⊤μ,σ2‖η‖2,[V−,V+]), with V± explicit. Inverting the truncated-normal CDF gives exact selective p-values and intervals.

The menu of fixes

ApproachGuaranteeCost
Data splittingExact, trivial to explainHalves power twice over; selection on half the data is worse
Selective inference (polyhedral)Exact conditional coverage, given the selection ruleRule must be pinned down; intervals can be infinite
Debiased lasso√n-consistent, asymptotically normal, per-coordinateNeeds sparse precision matrix; βˆd=βˆ+1nΘˆX⊤(y−Xβˆ)
PoSI (Berk et al.)Simultaneous over all submodelsVery conservative; constant grows like √2logp

The debiased lasso is the most useful of these in practice and the most transparent: the correction term is precisely a one-step Newton correction removing the ℓ1 bias, and the resulting √n(βˆdj−βj)⇒N(0,σ2[ΘˆΣˆΘˆ⊤]jj) holds provided the bias term √n‖(ΘˆΣˆ−I)(βˆ−β)‖∞=o(1), which needs s=o(√n/logp). That sparsity requirement is stronger than the one needed for estimation — inference costs more sparsity than estimation does.

Multiple Testing and Variable Selection Control

Day 41 · 85 min

Can we control FDR over selected variables in a regression, in finite samples, with no p-values at all?

Theorem — knockoff FDR control (Barber & Candès)

If for null j the statistic Wj is symmetric (swapping Xj and X˜j flips its sign) and the Wj satisfy the sufficiency property, then the selection rule above has 𝔼[|Sˆ∩ℋ0||Sˆ|∨1]≤q exactly, for any n and p<n (fixed-X knockoffs) or any n,p (model-X).

Model-X knockoffs shift the assumption: instead of knowing the conditional law y∣X (as classical inference does), you assume you know the covariate law PX. That is a genuinely different trade, and it is often the better one — covariates are abundant and unlabelled, responses are scarce. The construction requires sampling X˜ from a distribution matching all conditionals, exact for Gaussians and approximate otherwise; robustness to misspecified PX is the main practical caveat.

Related machinery. The conditional randomization test tests H0:Y⊥Xj∣X−j by resampling Xj from its conditional law — exact, and computationally heavier. e-values (nonnegative statistics with 𝔼[E]≤1 under the null) give FDR control via e-BH under arbitrary dependence, and combine by averaging rather than by Fisher/Stouffer rules. Storey's πˆ0 plug-in improves BH's power when many nulls are false; q-values are the FDR analogue of p-values.

Random Projections and Sketching

Day 42 · 85 min

Can you shrink n or p by an order of magnitude and keep a provable guarantee?

Theorem — Johnson–Lindenstrauss

For any x1..xN∈ℝd and ϵ∈(0,1), if k≥Cϵ−2logN and Π∈ℝk×d has i.i.d. N(0,1/k) entries, then w.p. ≥1−2/N, (1−ϵ)‖xi−xj‖2≤‖Πxi−Πxj‖2≤(1+ϵ)‖xi−xj‖2∀i,j.

proofFor fixed u, ‖Πu‖2/‖u‖2∼χ2k/k, which concentrates: ℙ(|χ2k/k−1|>ϵ)≤2e−kϵ2/8. Union bound over (N2) pairs and solve for k.

Notice what k does not depend on: the ambient dimension d. That is the striking part, and it is a direct consequence of concentration on the sphere (Lecture 52). Sparse JL (Kane–Nelson) achieves the same with O(ϵ−1logN) nonzeros per column; the SRHT applies Π in O(dlogd) time via an FFT.

Where it breaks

Objective-value guarantees are not coefficient guarantees: a sketch giving (1+ϵ) error in ‖Xβ−y‖ can leave βˆ far from β⋆ when the design is ill-conditioned, because the map from objective to argument amplifies by κ. If you need coefficients, use sketch-and-precondition, not sketch-and-solve. Uniform row sampling is also fatal when leverage is non-uniform — the whole point of leverage scores.

Nonparametric Rates and the Curse of Dimensionality

Day 42 · 75 min

Exactly how bad is the curse, and what structural assumption is the cheapest escape?

Theorem — minimax rate over Hölder balls

For f∈Cβ([0,1]d) with regression noise σ2, inffˆsupf𝔼‖fˆ−f‖22≍n−2β/(2β+d). Upper bound: local polynomial of degree ⌊β⌋ with bandwidth h≍n−1/(2β+d), balancing squared bias h2β against variance 1/(nhd). Lower bound: Fano over ≍h−d bump perturbations (Lecture 23).

Read the exponent as a sample-size requirement. To reach error ϵ you need n≍ϵ−(2β+d)/(2β). With β=2 and ϵ=0.1: d=1 needs ≈102.5, d=10 needs ≈107, d=20 needs ≈1012. The curse is not asymptotic pedantry; it bites at d=10.

Adaptivity: not knowing β is free

Lepski's method: compute estimates fˆh on a geometric grid of bandwidths, and select the largest h whose estimate is consistent with all smaller ones within their confidence radii. Achieves the oracle rate for the true β up to log factors, without knowing β. Wavelet thresholding (Donoho–Johnstone) achieves it by soft-thresholding wavelet coefficients at σ√2logn, adapting simultaneously over a whole Besov scale — and, unlike kernel methods, adapts to spatially varying smoothness.

Escapes from the curse — all are assumptions

AssumptionRateCost
Additive f=∑jfj(xj)n−2β/(2β+1), no d in the exponentNo interactions at all
Sparse additive, s activen−2β/(2β+1)+slogpnSpAM; needs sparsity

"More features provably hurts." Adding an irrelevant coordinate increases d in the exponent for a method that does not know it is irrelevant, degrading the rate from n−2β/(2β+d) to n−2β/(2β+d+1). Methods that adapt to the relevant set (trees with variable selection, sparse additive models, ARD kernels in Lecture 76) avoid this; kernel smoothers with an isotropic bandwidth do not.

Recitation 4: High-Dimensional Problem Set 🔴

  1. Verify RE numerically. For an equicorrelated Gaussian design, estimate minv∈𝒞(S)‖Xv‖2/(n‖v‖2) by projected gradient on the cone, and plot γ against ρ and n/s. Find where it collapses.
