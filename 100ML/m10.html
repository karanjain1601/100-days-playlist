Latent Variable Models & Unsupervised Theory

Reading: ESL Ch. 14 · PML2 Ch. 13–21 · von Luxburg · Anandkumar et al. Unsupervised learning has no loss to validate against, so identifiability replaces generalization as the central question. Every lecture here is about what is and is not recoverable in principle.

119

Latent Variable Models and Identifiability

Day 81 · 85 min
Identifiability

A model {pθ} is identifiable if pθ=pθ′⇒θ=θ′. For latent variable models this almost always fails without qualification, and the useful statements are of the form "identifiable up to 𝒢" for an explicit group 𝒢 (permutation, rotation, scaling).

Why it matters and when it does not. Identifiability is a precondition for interpretation, never for prediction. A non-identifiable model can predict perfectly; it just cannot support a claim about "the" third factor. Reviewers conflate the two constantly, in both directions.

ModelIdentifiable up toCondition
Finite mixturelabel permutationTeicher: the component family must be linearly independent; true for Gaussian, Poisson, gamma; false for Bernoulli (a mixture of Bernoullis on one variable is a Bernoulli) and for uniforms
Factor analysisrotation of the loading matrixNeeds p≥ Ledermann bound; the subspace is identified, the loadings are not
ICApermutation & scalingAt most one Gaussian source (Comon)
Topic model / NMFpermutationSeparability / anchor words
HMMstate permutationDistinct emissions, ergodic transitions

Label switching is the practical face of permutation non-identifiability. A correctly mixing MCMC chain on a mixture must visit all K! modes, so posterior means of component-specific parameters converge to the same value for every component. Fixes: order constraints (crude, distorts the posterior), post-hoc relabelling by minimizing a loss over permutations (Stephens), or reporting only permutation-invariant functionals (the density, the partition, pairwise co-clustering probabilities). The last is the principled option.

Method of moments and tensor decomposition

For exchangeable models with a k-dimensional latent h and three conditionally independent views x1,x2,x3 with 𝔼[xv∣h=j]=μj, the third moment is a low-rank tensor M3=𝔼[x1⊗x2⊗x3]=k∑j=1wjμj⊗μj⊗μj. Kruskal's theorem: a CP decomposition ∑jaj⊗bj⊗cj is unique (up to permutation and scaling) if kA+kB+kC≥2R+2 where k denotes Kruskal rank. For μj linearly independent this holds — hence third-order moments identify the model, while second-order moments (a matrix) never do, because matrix factorizations have a rotational ambiguity that tensors do not.

The algorithm. Whiten M2 so the components become orthonormal, then run tensor power iteration on the whitened M3: robustly recovers all components with polynomial sample complexity. This yields consistent, non-iterative, initialization-free estimators for mixtures of Gaussians, HMMs, LDA, and multi-view models — the main alternative to EM, and one with actual guarantees. Its weakness is statistical efficiency: third moments are noisy, so spectral estimates are typically used to initialize EM rather than to replace it.

Mixture Models and EM Theory

Day 82 · 85 min
The likelihood is unbounded

For a Gaussian mixture with free covariances, set μ1=x1 and σ1→0: the density at x1 diverges and so does the likelihood. The global MLE does not exist. What practitioners call "the MLE" is a particular local maximum, and its existence relies on constraints (variance floor, shared covariance, or a prior/penalty). Any paper reporting "the maximum likelihood fit of a GMM" is reporting an EM fixed point.

EM for GMMs. E-step: responsibilities γik=πkN(xi;μk,Σk)∑lπlN(xi;μl,Σl). M-step: πk=γk, μk=∑iγikxi∑iγik, Σk=∑iγik(xi−μk)(xi−μk)⊤∑iγik — weighted moment matching, exactly as Lecture 12 predicts.

Theorem — EM's basin of attraction (Balakrishnan, Wainwright & Yu)

For a two-component symmetric Gaussian mixture with separation ‖μ‖/σ≥C, if θ0 lies within a ball of radius ‖μ‖/4 of the truth, then population EM contracts geometrically and sample EM satisfies ‖θt−θ⋆‖≤κt‖θ0−θ⋆‖+C′1−κ√dn, i.e. geometric convergence to a ball of the minimax radius. Outside the basin no guarantee holds, and bad fixed points genuinely exist.

Statistical–computational gap. Information-theoretically, two Gaussians are distinguishable with separation Ω(1) and n≳d samples. Computationally, polynomial-time algorithms are known under separation Ω(√logk) (and, with recent SoS methods, at separation kϵ with quasi-polynomial time); below that, SQ lower bounds (Diakonikolas–Kane–Stewart) rule out a large class of efficient algorithms. This is the cleanest mixture-model instance of Lecture 38's theme.

Initialization matters more than the algorithm. k-means++ seeding, spectral/tensor initialization, or many random restarts with the best likelihood retained. Reporting a single-run EM result without restarts is not a reproducible experiment.

Choosing K. BIC (penalizes dKlogn, tends to overestimate under misspecification because extra components absorb non-Gaussianity); ICL adds the expected entropy of the classification, favouring well-separated components and typically choosing fewer; Bayesian nonparametric approaches (Lecture 114) do not solve it either. The honest framing: "how many components" is only a well-posed question if you believe the mixture is the data-generating process; otherwise it is a modelling choice to be made on downstream criteria.

Mixtures of experts replace fixed weights with gating πk(x): p(y∣x)=∑kπk(x)pk(y∣x). Jointly this is still an EM problem, and it is the classical ancestor of every conditional-computation architecture.

Factor Analysis, PPCA and Linear Latent Models

Day 82 · 85 min
Models

Factor analysis: x=Wz+μ+ε, z∼N(0,Ik), ε∼N(0,Ψ) with Ψ diagonal. Hence Cov(x)=WW⊤+Ψ.
PPCA: the same with Ψ=σ2I.

Rotation indeterminacy. W→WR for orthogonal R leaves WW⊤ unchanged, so only the column space of W is identified. Varimax and friends choose a rotation by an interpretability criterion, not by evidence; "factor 2 is verbal ability" is a claim about the rotation you chose. Heywood cases — negative estimated Ψjj — signal too many factors, a near-singular solution, or genuinely non-diagonal residual structure; they occur often enough that any FA implementation must handle them explicitly.

Theorem — the PPCA maximum likelihood solution (Tipping & Bishop)

With S the sample covariance and eigenpairs (λj,uj), WML=Uk(Λk−σ2I)1/2R,σ2ML=1p−k∑j>kλj. The principal subspace emerges from maximum likelihood; the residual variance is the average discarded eigenvalue.

Two consequences. As σ2→0, PPCA's posterior mean projection 𝔼[z∣x]=(W⊤W+σ2I)−1W⊤(x−μ) converges to the orthogonal PCA projection — PCA is the zero-noise limit of a probabilistic model. And FA is not PCA with noise: FA's diagonal-but-unequal Ψ makes it invariant to rescaling of individual variables, while PCA is not. FA is the right model when variables have different measurement precisions; PCA when they are commensurate.

EM for PPCA costs O(npk) per iteration versus O(np2) for the covariance eigendecomposition — the reason probabilistic formulations win in high dimension, and it handles missing data natively by treating the missing entries as additional latents.

Probabilistic CCA (Bach–Jordan): two views with a shared latent z and view-specific latents recovers classical CCA directions as the ML solution, and gives the Bayesian machinery for regularization and for missing views. Exponential-family PCA replaces the Gaussian likelihood with any exponential family (Bernoulli for binary data, Poisson for counts), which is the correct treatment of binary or count matrices — applying ordinary PCA to binary data is a modelling error, not merely an approximation.

PCA: What Is Actually True

Day 83 · 85 min

Three derivations, one answer. (i) Maximize variance: max‖u‖=1u⊤Su — Rayleigh quotient, Lecture 6. (ii) Minimize reconstruction error: minrankk‖X−Xˆ‖F — Eckart–Young, Lecture 7. (iii) Maximum likelihood under PPCA — Lecture 121. That three quite different criteria agree is the reason PCA is canonical, and it is specific to the Gaussian/L2 world; change the loss and they diverge.

Consistency, and its failure

Fixed p, n→∞: uˆ1→u1 a.s., with √n(uˆ1−u1) asymptotically normal with variance ∑j≠1λ1λj(λ1−λj)2ujuj⊤ — note the eigengap in the denominator (Davis–Kahan again).
p/n→γ>0: by BBP (Lecture 52), |⟨uˆ1,u1⟩|2→(1−γℓ2)/(1+γℓ) for a spike ℓ>√γ, and →0 below the threshold. Sample PCA is inconsistent in high dimensions, and no amount of care in computing the eigenvector fixes it.

Sparse PCA. Assume u1 is s-sparse. The optimal estimator attains √slogp/n, but the natural formulation maxu⊤Su s.t. ‖u‖0≤s is NP-hard. Convex relaxations (d'Aspremont et al.'s SDP; Amini–Wainwright's analysis) succeed for n≳s2logp, while the information-theoretic threshold is slogp — a genuine statistical–computational gap, with matching lower bounds under the planted-clique conjecture. Diagonal thresholding is a cheap, provably good screening step.

Robust PCA (Lecture 55) handles gross corruption; classical PCA has breakdown point 0, so a single extreme point can rotate the leading component arbitrarily.

Questions PCA cannot answer
  • Scaling. PCA on a covariance matrix is dominated by high-variance variables; on a correlation matrix it treats all variables as equally important. Neither is "correct" — the choice is a modelling decision that changes the answer, and it must be stated.
  • How many components. Scree plots must be read against the Marchenko–Pastur null, not against a flat line; parallel analysis (compare to permuted data) is the practical version.
  • Sign and rotation. Signs are arbitrary; within a near-tied block, only the subspace is meaningful. Interpretations of individual loadings in a tied block are unsupported.
  • Supervision. The top principal directions need not carry the signal; a small-variance direction may be the only predictive one. PCA-then-regress is a heuristic with no optimality property, and partial least squares or supervised PCA answer the actual question.

Independent Component Analysis

Day 83 · 85 min

The problem. x=As with s having independent, non-Gaussian components. Recover A and s from x alone.

Why decorrelation is not enough. Whitening gives Cov(z)=I, but any rotation Rz is also white — second-order statistics leave an orthogonal ambiguity of dimension k(k−1)/2. Independence is strictly stronger than uncorrelatedness, and fixing the rotation requires higher-order information.

Theorem — Comon's identifiability

If at most one component of s is Gaussian, then A is identifiable up to permutation and scaling of its columns. If two or more are Gaussian, their subspace is identifiable but their individual directions are not.

why Gaussianity is fatalA rotation of an isotropic Gaussian is an isotropic Gaussian; independence is preserved. So within a Gaussian block there is no statistic that distinguishes rotations. (Darmois–Skitovich makes the converse precise: if two independent linear combinations of independent variables are independent, the variables involved are Gaussian.)

Contrast functions

  • Kurtosis kurt(y)=𝔼y4−3: simple and differentiable, but the fourth moment is extremely sensitive to outliers.
  • Negentropy J(y)=H(ygauss)−H(y)≥0 with equality iff Gaussian — the principled contrast. Approximated by J(y)≈[𝔼G(y)−𝔼G(ν)]2 with G(u)=logcosh(au) or −e−u2/2, both far more robust than kurtosis.
  • Mutual information I(y1..yk)=∑jH(yj)−H(y); after whitening, H(y) is constant, so minimizing I equals maximizing total negentropy. All contrasts are the same objective in different coordinates.
FastICA

Fixed-point iteration for one component: w←𝔼[xg(w⊤x)]−𝔼[g′(w⊤x)]w, then normalize; deflate or symmetrically orthogonalize for multiple components. It is a Newton method on the negentropy contrast under whitening, hence cubically convergent locally — far faster than gradient-based infomax.

Infomax and ML. Maximizing the output entropy of a nonlinear network with squashing g equals maximum likelihood with source density ∝g′; ICA is thus an ML method with an assumed (super- or sub-Gaussian) source density, and it is robust to getting that density roughly right — only the sign of the kurtosis must match.

Nonlinear ICA is impossible without extra structure

Hyvärinen & Pajunen: for x=f(s) with f an arbitrary invertible nonlinearity, there are infinitely many decompositions into independent components — independence alone carries no information. Identifiability is restored by an auxiliary variable u (time index, segment label, class) such that the sources are conditionally independent given u and sufficiently modulated by it (TCL, iVAE, permutation-contrastive learning). This result is the foundation of modern causal representation learning (Lecture 144), and it is why claims of "disentanglement" without auxiliary structure are unidentifiable in principle — the point of Locatello et al.'s impossibility theorem.

Clustering Theory

Day 84 · 85 min
Theorem — Kleinberg's impossibility

No clustering function on finite distance data satisfies all three of:
Scale invariance — f(αd)=f(d) for α>0;
Richness — every partition is achievable for some d;
Consistency — shrinking within-cluster distances and stretching between-cluster distances leaves the output unchanged.

sketchRichness plus consistency force an "antichain" structure on achievable partitions; scale invariance then collapses it. Each pair of axioms is satisfiable: single-linkage with a k-cluster stopping rule satisfies scale invariance and consistency but not richness; with a distance-r stopping rule it satisfies richness and consistency but not scale invariance.

The moral is not that clustering is impossible but that clustering is under-specified. The axioms are individually reasonable and jointly inconsistent, so any algorithm has made a choice — usually about scale. Ackerman & Ben-David show the impossibility dissolves for clustering quality measures rather than functions, which is a more useful formalization.

k-means: hardness and guarantees

Minimizing ∑i‖xi−μci‖2 is NP-hard even for k=2 in general dimension, and even in the plane for general k. Lloyd's algorithm decreases the objective monotonically (each step is an exact minimization over one block) and terminates finitely, but can be arbitrarily bad and can take 2Ω(√n) iterations in the worst case (smoothed complexity is polynomial).

Theorem — k-means++ (Arthur & Vassilvitskii)

Seed the first centre uniformly, then each subsequent centre with probability proportional to D(x)2, the squared distance to the nearest chosen centre. Then 𝔼[ϕ]≤8(lnk+2)ϕOPT.

sketchTwo lemmas: (i) if a cluster of the optimal solution is "hit" by a D2-sampled centre, its expected contribution is at most 8× its optimal cost; (ii) an inductive argument bounds the cost of the clusters never hit, contributing the lnk. The D2 weighting is exactly what makes far-away optimal clusters likely to be hit.

k-means, k-means++ and spectral clustering on the same data

Clusterability assumptions. The tractable regimes are all "if the data are clearly clustered, clustering is easy": ϵ-separation (ϕk≤ϵ2ϕk−1, Ostrovsky et al. — then Lloyd from a good seed is near-optimal); perturbation resilience (the optimal partition is unchanged under multiplicative distance perturbations up to α — solvable exactly for α≥2, Awasthi–Blum–Sheffet); approximation stability (all near-optimal solutions are close to the optimum). The theory is honest about it: there is no algorithm that finds structure that is not clearly there.

Choosing k. Stability-based selection (Ben-Hur, Lange et al.): cluster subsamples and measure agreement; choose the k maximizing it. It has known pathologies — stability can peak at k=2 regardless of structure — so use it alongside the gap statistic and downstream criteria, not alone.

Relation to GMM. k-means is the σ→0 limit of EM for a GMM with isotropic, equal covariances and equal weights — hard assignments replace responsibilities. That is why k-means finds spherical, equal-sized clusters and misreads elongated ones.

Spectral Methods and Graph-Based Learning

Day 84 · 85 min
Laplacians

L=D−W (unnormalized), Lsym=D−1/2LD−1/2, Lrw=D−1L=I−D−1W. Key identity: f⊤Lf=12∑i,jwij(fi−fj)2≥0, so L⪰0 and it measures smoothness of f on the graph.

Theorem — multiplicity of the zero eigenvalue

The multiplicity of eigenvalue 0 of L equals the number of connected components, and the eigenspace is spanned by the components' indicator vectors.

proofLf=0⇒f⊤Lf=0⇒fi=fj whenever wij>0, so f is constant on components; conversely each indicator is in the kernel.

Relaxation of cuts. RatioCut and NCut are NP-hard; writing them as trace minimizations over constrained indicator matrices and relaxing the constraint to orthonormality gives minH⊤H=Itr(H⊤LH), solved by the bottom eigenvectors (of L for RatioCut, of Lrw or Lsym for NCut). Then run k-means on the rows of H — the relaxed solution is continuous and must be rounded.

Cheeger's inequality

λ22≤hG≤√2λ2 where hG is the conductance. So a small spectral gap certifies a good cut and a large gap certifies none exists — this is the quantitative reason spectral clustering works, and also the reason it fails on graphs with no bottleneck.

Consistency (von Luxburg, Belkin & Bousquet). As n→∞, normalized spectral clustering converges to a sensible limit partition of the underlying distribution; unnormalized spectral clustering converges only under an extra condition (λ<minidi), and otherwise its eigenvectors converge to spikes on individual outliers. Use the normalized Laplacian — the choice is not cosmetic.

Stochastic block model. n nodes, k communities, within/between probabilities a/n,b/n. Exact recovery is possible iff √a−√b√2>√logn-type thresholds (Abbe et al.); detection (beating chance) is possible iff (a−b)2>2(a+b) — the Kesten–Stigum threshold, below which no algorithm can do better than random, and at which belief propagation is conjectured (and in cases proved) optimal. Spectral methods on the non-backtracking matrix achieve the threshold where the adjacency matrix does not, because the adjacency spectrum is contaminated by high-degree nodes.

Semi-supervised learning on graphs. Label propagation minimizes f⊤Lf subject to fixed labelled values — a harmonic function with fu=−Luu−1Lulfl. It is a Gaussian random field, and the same object as GP regression with the graph Laplacian as precision (Lecture 81). Its known failure: in high dimension with few labels, the harmonic solution degenerates to a nearly constant function with spikes at the labelled points (Nadler, Srebro & Zhou) — the graph-Laplacian regularizer is not strong enough in d≥3 without modification (p-Laplacian, higher-order regularizers).

The manifold hypothesis. Evidence for: natural images and audio have intrinsic dimension estimates far below ambient; local PCA on patches shows low-rank structure. Evidence against: real data are usually a union of manifolds of differing dimension with noise off-manifold, so the smooth-compact-manifold idealization is a convenience. Rates depending on intrinsic d0 (Lecture 59) are the reason it matters at all.

What t-SNE plots do and do not show

t-SNE's objective is KL(P‖Q) over neighbourhood distributions, with the Student-t kernel in the embedding chosen to alleviate the crowding problem — in d dimensions, the volume available at radius r grows like rd, so a faithful 2-D embedding of high-dimensional neighbourhoods has nowhere to put the moderately-distant points. Consequences, all documented:

  • Cluster sizes are meaningless — the algorithm expands dense clusters and contracts sparse ones.
  • Between-cluster distances are meaningless — only local structure is optimized.
  • Perplexity changes the output qualitatively; separate runs give different pictures.
  • t-SNE will produce apparent clusters from pure isotropic noise at small perplexity.
UMAP is faster and preserves somewhat more global structure but shares the fundamental limitation; its theoretical framing in terms of fuzzy simplicial sets does not amount to a recovery guarantee. Never draw a scientific conclusion from a t-SNE or UMAP layout alone. Use it to generate hypotheses and confirm them with a method that has a guarantee — including checking whether the clusters survive in the original space.

Intrinsic dimension estimation. Correlation dimension, MLE (Levina–Bickel), TwoNN. All are biased downward at high d and sensitive to noise; report them with uncertainty and across scales.

Nonnegative Matrix Factorization and Topic Models

Day 85 · 85 min

NMF: X≈WH with W,H≥0. Exact NMF is NP-hard (Vavasis), and the solution is generally non-unique: any invertible Q with WQ≥0 and Q−1H≥0 gives another factorization. Nonnegativity restricts but does not eliminate the rotational ambiguity of Lecture 121.

Separability restores uniqueness

X is separable if every topic has an anchor word — a row of W that is a positive multiple of a coordinate vector, i.e. a word occurring only in that topic. Under separability, NMF is identifiable and solvable in polynomial time (Arora, Ge, Kannan & Moitra): find the anchors as the vertices of the convex hull of the row-normalized word–word co-occurrence matrix, then recover the topics by a convex program.

Multiplicative updates (Lee & Seung) H←H⊙W⊤XW⊤WH are an MM algorithm: they monotonically decrease the divergence. Two caveats routinely omitted: monotone decrease does not imply convergence to a stationary point (zeros are absorbing — a coordinate that hits 0 can never return), and the updates are undefined when a denominator vanishes. Alternating nonnegative least squares (block coordinate descent with projection) has proper stationarity guarantees and is usually faster.

Latent Dirichlet allocation

θd∼Dir(α); for each word, zdn∼Cat(θd), wdn∼Cat(βzdn). Two standard inference routes:

  • Collapsed Gibbs. Integrate out θ,β; then p(zdn=k∣⋅)∝n(d)k,¬+αk∑k′n(d)k′,¬+α⋅n(k)w,¬+η∑w′n(k)w′,¬+Vη — a product of a document-topic and a topic-word ratio, each a Pólya urn. Simple, memory-light, and the Rao-Blackwellization of Lecture 101 in action.
  • Mean-field VI. q(θ)q(z)q(β) with CAVI updates ϕdnk∝exp{𝔼logθdk+𝔼logβkw}, where 𝔼logθdk=ψ(γdk)−ψ(∑γd) (digamma) — the exponential-family identity of Lecture 12 again. SVI makes it scale to streaming corpora.

Identifiability of topic models holds under anchor-word or sufficient-diversity conditions, and under an α<1 sparsity condition on the topic proportions (Ke & Wang). Without them, "the topics" are not a well-defined estimand.

Held-out likelihood vs coherence

Chang et al.'s "Reading tea leaves" showed the two anti-correlate: models with better held-out perplexity produced topics humans judged less interpretable. Coherence measures (NPMI, Cv) correlate better with human judgment. So report both, and be clear which one your claim rests on — a model chosen by perplexity is not a model that will produce readable topics.

Recitation 10: Unsupervised Learning Problem Set

Day 86 · 60 min
  1. Identifiability of a mixture family. Prove finite mixtures of Poissons are identifiable (use the generating function / linear independence of e−λλk), and then show mixtures of Bernoullis on a single binary variable are not.
  2. EM for a non-Gaussian mixture. Derive the E- and M-steps for a mixture of Laplace densities and for a mixture of von Mises distributions; note where the M-step loses its closed form and what to do about it.
  3. k-means++ empirically. Verify the O(logk) approximation guarantee on adversarial instances (widely separated clusters of very different sizes), and construct an instance where uniform seeding is Ω(k) worse.
  4. Graph Laplacian by hand. For a two-block graph with n1,n2 nodes and within/between weights a,b, compute the full spectrum analytically; verify the Fiedler vector separates the blocks and find the b at which the gap closes.
  5. t-SNE on noise. Run t-SNE on i.i.d. Gaussian data at several perplexities and show that apparent clusters appear; then run the same data through PCA and confirm nothing is there.
Two papers

Foundational: von Luxburg (2007), A tutorial on spectral clustering — §8 on the normalized/unnormalized distinction is the part to read closely. Modern: Anandkumar et al. (2014), Tensor decompositions for learning latent variable models.
