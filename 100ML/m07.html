Nonparametric Regression Foundations

Day 59 · 85 min
Nadaraya–Watson, derived

m(x)=𝔼[Y∣X=x]=∫yf(x,y)dyf(x). Substitute kernel density estimates for numerator and denominator: mˆh(x)=∑iK(x−Xih)Yi∑iK(x−Xih)=∑iwi(x)Yi, a linear smoother: Yˆ=ShY with ∑jSij=1.

Bias–variance, exactly

Interior point, second-order kernel, μ2=∫u2K, R(K)=∫K2: Bias=h2μ22(m′′(x)+2m′(x)f′(x)f(x))+o(h2),Var=σ2R(K)nhf(x)+o(1nh). MISE-optimal h≍n−1/5, giving MISE ≍n−4/5 — the β=2,d=1 case of Lecture 59.

The design-bias term. The 2m′f′/f piece is bias caused purely by the density of the design, not by curvature of m. It is why NW is badly biased at the boundary (where f drops off) and in sparse regions. Local linear regression — fit mina,b∑iKh(x−Xi)(Yi−a−b(Xi−x))2 and report aˆ — removes it entirely: its bias is h2μ22m′′(x), free of f, at both interior and boundary points. This is automatic boundary correction, and it is the reason local linear (degree 1, or degree ⌊β⌋ generally) should be the default over NW.

Bandwidth, bias and variance — local constant vs local linear

Choosing h. LOOCV via the smoother identity CV(h)=1n∑(Yi−mˆh(Xi)1−Sii)2 (Lecture 43's PRESS again); GCV replaces Sii by tr(S)/n. Plug-in rules estimate ∫(m′′)2 in a pilot stage and are less variable but more assumption-laden. Effective degrees of freedom =tr(Sh) makes any linear smoother comparable to a parametric fit on the same axis.

k-Nearest Neighbours: Theory

Day 60 · 85 min
Theorem — Stone (universal consistency)

If k→∞ and k/n→0, the k-NN regression estimate is L2-consistent for every distribution with 𝔼Y2<∞ — no smoothness, no density assumption. The same holds for classification risk →R⋆.

This is a remarkable statement and it is the reason k-NN is the canonical "assumption-free" method. The price is entirely in the rate: under β-Hölder smoothness the risk is O(n−2β/(2β+d)) with k≍n2β/(2β+d), i.e. optimal but cursed.

Theorem — Cover & Hart

As n→∞, the 1-NN classification risk satisfies R⋆≤R1NN≤2R⋆(1−R⋆)≤2R⋆.

proofAs n→∞ the nearest neighbour's covariate converges to x, so its label is drawn from η(x) independently of the query's label. Then P(error∣x)→2η(1−η), while the Bayes error is min(η,1−η). Take expectations and apply Jensen to the concave map.

Read it as: half the information is in the single nearest label. With the Tsybakov margin condition (ℙ(|η−12|≤t)≲tα), k-NN attains the fast rate n−β(α+1)2β+d — the same interpolation between 1/√n and 1/n seen in Lecture 38.

The curse, made precise. The expected distance to the nearest of n uniform points in [0,1]d is ≍n−1/d. To have neighbours within distance 0.1 in d=20 you need n≈1020. Equivalently: the fraction of the unit cube within 0.1 of a fixed point is 10−20. Every "local" method is non-local in high dimension.

Remedies. Metric learning (LMNN, NCA) learns a Mahalanobis metric dA(x,y)=(x−y)⊤A(x−y) so that the effective dimension is the rank of A; this converts k-NN into a two-stage procedure whose first stage must be validated separately. ANN structures: KD-trees are exact but degrade to linear scan past d≈20; LSH gives sublinear query with a (1+ϵ)-approximation guarantee; HNSW graphs are the current practical standard with excellent recall/latency but no worst-case guarantee. Approximation error in the neighbour search adds to statistical error and is rarely reported.

Splines and Additive Models

Day 60 · 85 min
Theorem — the smoothing spline

Among all f∈W2,2[a,b], the minimizer of n∑i=1(yi−f(xi))2+λ∫abf′′(t)2dt is the natural cubic spline with knots at the unique xi — a finite-dimensional object, though the problem was posed over an infinite-dimensional space.

proofLet g be the natural cubic spline interpolating f at the xi and h=f−g. The fit term is identical. Integrate ∫g′′h′′ by parts twice: boundary terms vanish by naturalness (g′′=0 at the ends) and g′′′ is piecewise constant with ∫g′′′h′=∑jg′′′j∫knoth′=0 since h vanishes at knots. Hence ∫f′′2=∫g′′2+∫h′′2≥∫g′′2.

Note this is the representer theorem (Lecture 75) in disguise: W2,2 with that penalty is an RKHS, and the reproducing kernel is the cubic-spline Green's function. Two literatures, one theorem.

Bases. Truncated power basis {1,x,x2,x3,(x−ξj)+3} is intuitive and numerically terrible (nearly collinear). B-splines have local support, are numerically stable, and give banded normal equations — always use them. Knot placement at quantiles of x; for penalized splines, use many knots and let λ do the work (P-splines).

Solution and df. fˆ=(I+λK)−1y=Sλy, a symmetric smoother whose eigenvalues are 1/(1+λdj) with dj the eigenvalues of the penalty. The first two eigenvectors (constant and linear) have dj=0 and are never shrunk — the penalty's null space is exactly the linear functions. df=tr(Sλ).

GAMs and backfitting

𝔼[Y∣X]=α+∑jfj(Xj). Backfitting: cycle fj←Sj(y−α−∑k≠jfk), centering each fj. This is Gauss–Seidel on the normal equations of the additive model; it converges when the smoothers are symmetric with eigenvalues in [0,1] and the component spaces are not too concordant. Rate: n−2β/(2β+1) — the one-dimensional rate, in any d. That dimension-free exponent is the entire selling point, and it is bought with the no-interactions assumption.

MARS adds interactions adaptively via forward stagewise selection of hinge products, then prunes by GCV — a bridge between splines and trees. Thin-plate splines generalize the penalty to ∫‖∇2f‖F2 in ℝd with no knot placement needed; tensor-product splines build interactions on a grid but cost O(kd) basis functions.

Density Estimation

Day 61 · 85 min
KDE bias–variance

fˆh(x)=1nhd∑iK(x−Xih). For a second-order kernel: Bias=h2μ22∇2f(x)+o(h2), Var=f(x)R(K)nhd+o(1nhd). MISE-optimal h≍n−1/(d+4), MISE ≍n−4/(d+4). In d=1 Silverman's rule h=1.06σˆn−1/5 is the AMISE-optimal bandwidth if the truth is Gaussian; it oversmooths multimodal densities, and 0.9min(σˆ,IQR/1.34)n−1/5 is the standard robustification.

Boundary bias. On [0,∞) the kernel puts mass outside the support, so 𝔼fˆ(0)→f(0)/2 — a 50% error that does not shrink with n. Fixes: reflection, boundary kernels, log/logit transform, or local-linear density estimation. Any KDE plotted on a bounded variable without a boundary correction is wrong at the ends, and this shows up constantly in applied figures.

Curse. n−4/(d+4): reaching MISE 10−2 needs n≈103 at d=2 and ≈1012 at d=10. Density estimation is the hardest of the standard nonparametric tasks — harder than regression, because there is no response to average.

Alternatives when KDE fails

  • k-NN density: fˆ(x)=knVdRk(x)d — adaptive bandwidth, but not integrable to 1 and heavy-tailed.
  • Mixtures: parametric-rate estimation if the family is right; identifiability caveats from Lecture 119.
  • Shape constraints. Log-concave MLE exists, is unique, needs no tuning parameter, and attains n−2/(d+4) in Hellinger for d≤3. For unimodal-ish continuous data this is the most under-used estimator in the module.
  • Copulas. Sklar: F(x1..xd)=C(F1(x1),..,Fd(xd)) with C unique on the range of the margins. Estimate margins nonparametrically and C parametrically (Gaussian, t, vine) — a genuine dimension reduction that separates marginal behaviour from dependence.

Which loss? L2 is analytically convenient; L1 (total variation) is scale-invariant and has the interpretation supA|P(A)−Pˆ(A)|; Hellinger tensorizes and is the right metric for likelihood-based rates (Lecture 110). Rates differ, and a method optimal in one can be inadmissible in another.

Classification and Regression Trees

Day 62 · 85 min

Recursive binary partitioning: at each node choose (j,s) minimizing the weighted child impurity, and recurse. Prediction is constant on each leaf.

Why impurity must be strictly concave

Let ϕ be the node impurity as a function of the class distribution p. A split into children with weights wL,wR and distributions pL,pR (so p=wLpL+wRpR) reduces impurity iff ϕ(wLpL+wRpR)≥wLϕ(pL)+wRϕ(pR) — i.e. iff ϕ is concave, strictly so for the reduction to be positive whenever pL≠pR.

Impurityϕ(p) (binary)Behaviour
Misclassificationmin(p,1−p)Concave but not strictly — piecewise linear. Splits that purify without changing the majority class score zero. Useless for growing.
Gini2p(1−p)Strictly concave; equals the variance of the class indicator; equals the expected error of a randomized classifier
Entropy−plogp−(1−p)log(1−p)Strictly concave; steeper near 0 and 1, so slightly more eager to isolate pure nodes

Hence the standard practice: grow with Gini or entropy, prune with misclassification error. That is not an arbitrary convention — it follows from the concavity argument.

Theorem — cost-complexity pruning (Breiman et al.)

For Rα(T)=R(T)+α|T|, the minimizing subtrees form a nested, finite sequence T0⊃T1⊃…⊃{root} indexed by breakpoints 0=α0<α1<…. Weakest-link pruning — repeatedly collapse the internal node minimizing R(t)−R(Tt)|Tt|−1 — enumerates the whole sequence in one pass.

This is exactly structural risk minimization (Lecture 30) with an explicit, computable hierarchy — one of the few places where the SRM template is used literally in practice.

Missing values. Surrogate splits: at each node, rank alternative variables by how well their split reproduces the primary split's partition, and use the best available one when the primary is missing. This is strictly better than mean-imputation because it uses the local structure, and it is a distinctive advantage of CART-family methods.

Where single trees break

Instability: a small data perturbation changes an early split and the whole downstream structure — high variance, low bias, which is precisely the profile bagging fixes (next lecture). Axis alignment: a 45° boundary needs a staircase of splits. Greedy myopia: XOR is invisible to a single-variable split search. Consistency: partitioning estimators are consistent if the cells shrink and contain many points (Devroye–Györfi–Lugosi), but CART's data-dependent splits break the standard proof; consistency for CART itself was only established under restrictive conditions (Scornet–Biau–Vert).

Bagging and Random Forests

Day 63 · 85 min
Why bagging works — and when it cannot

For B predictors each with variance σ2 and pairwise correlation ρ, Var(1B∑bfˆb)=ρσ2+1−ρBσ2B→∞⟶ρσ2. So averaging removes only the uncorrelated part; the floor is ρσ2. Random forests attack ρ directly by sampling m of p features at each split.

Bagging cannot help a linear predictor. If fˆ is linear in y (fˆ=Sy) and the bootstrap is exchangeable, the bagged predictor converges to (approximately) the same Sy — the averaging has nothing nonlinear to smooth. Bagging is a variance reduction for unstable, nonlinear procedures; applying it to OLS or ridge is wasted compute, and this is a standard exam question because it isolates the mechanism.

Theorem — Breiman's forest bound

Generalization error of a random forest satisfies PE⋆≤ρ(1−s2)s2 where s is the strength (expected margin of a single tree) and ρ the mean correlation between trees' margin functions. As B→∞ the error converges a.s. — forests do not overfit in B.

The bound is loose but it names the trade correctly: reducing m decorrelates trees (lowers ρ) but weakens each (lowers s). Defaults m=√p (classification), p/3 (regression) come from this balance empirically.

Out-of-bag error. Each bootstrap sample omits ≈e−1=36.8% of points; predicting each point using only the trees that did not see it gives a nearly unbiased risk estimate for free. It is slightly pessimistic (each point is predicted by ≈0.37B trees rather than B) and it is not a substitute for a test set when hyperparameters are tuned on it.

Consistency and the kernel view. Consistency is proved for simplified forests: centred forests (splits at random midpoints) and median forests are consistent with rates (Biau); Scornet–Biau–Vert give consistency for Breiman's forests under an additive model. More illuminating: a forest's prediction is ∑iwi(x)yi with wi(x)=1B∑b𝟙[Xi∈Lb(x)]|Lb(x)| — an adaptive nearest-neighbour / kernel method whose metric is learned from the response. That is the right mental model, and it is exactly what causal forests (Lecture 142) exploit.

Variable importance is biased

Impurity-based (Gini) importance is biased toward high-cardinality and continuous variables, because they offer more candidate split points and can reduce impurity by chance (Strobl et al.). Demonstration: add a pure-noise variable with 1000 levels and it will outrank a genuine binary predictor. Permutation importance avoids that bias but has its own: permuting a correlated feature creates off-manifold inputs, so the model is evaluated where it was never trained. Conditional permutation importance permutes within strata of correlated variables and is the defensible default; even so, importance is a statement about this model, not about the data-generating process (Lecture 95).

Boosting I: The Theory of AdaBoost

Day 64 · 85 min
AdaBoost

Initialize wi=1/n. For t=1..T: fit ht to weighted data; compute ϵt=∑iwi𝟙[ht(xi)≠yi]; set αt=12log1−ϵtϵt; update wi∝wie−αtyiht(xi). Output sign(∑tαtht).

Theorem — training error decays exponentially

If ϵt≤12−γ for all t, then the training error of the combined classifier is at most ∏t2√ϵt(1−ϵt)≤e−2γ2T.

proofThe 0–1 loss is bounded by the exponential loss, and the normalization constants telescope: 1n∑i𝟙[yiF(xi)≤0]≤1n∑ie−yiF(xi)=∏tZt. Minimizing Zt=∑iwie−αtyiht over αt yields exactly the stated αt and Zt=2√ϵt(1−ϵt).

AdaBoost is forward stagewise additive modelling under exponential loss (Friedman–Hastie–Tibshirani): at each round it solves minα,h∑ie−yi(Ft−1(xi)+αh(xi)). The population minimizer of exponential loss is F⋆(x)=12logη(x)1−η(x) — half the log-odds — so AdaBoost is estimating a logit, and ηˆ=1/(1+e−2F) is the implied probability. Recognizing this converted boosting from an algorithmic curiosity into a statistical model in 2000, and it is the move that makes Lecture 93 possible.

The overfitting puzzle. Test error often keeps falling after training error reaches zero — impossible if capacity grew with T. The margin explanation: the normalized margin distribution keeps improving, and the bound R(f)≤Rˆθn(f)+O˜(√d/(nθ2)) is independent of T. Breiman's arc-gv maximizes the minimum margin directly, generalized worse, and was the strongest counterargument; Reyzin–Schapire showed arc-gv produced deeper trees, confounding the comparison. Current view: the whole margin distribution matters, not its minimum, and boosting's implicit regularization (small αt, shallow learners) is essential.

Label noise

Exponential loss grows as e−yF, so a persistently misclassified point receives exponentially growing weight. With mislabelled data, AdaBoost concentrates the entire weight mass on the noise. LogitBoost (logistic loss, linear growth) and Gentle/Robust variants degrade gracefully. If your labels are noisy, the exponential loss is the wrong choice and no amount of early stopping fully repairs it.

Boosting II: Gradient Boosting and Its Modern Descendants

Day 64 · 85 min

Shrinkage. ν and T trade off almost exactly: halving ν requires roughly doubling T for the same fit, and smaller ν generalizes better. There is no free lunch — ν small enough approximates ℓ1-regularized fitting over the space of trees (the "boosting path is close to the lasso path" result of Efron et al. / Rosset–Zhu–Hastie).

Stochastic gradient boosting (subsample rows per iteration) reduces variance and cost simultaneously; typical 0.5–0.8.

SystemKey ideasTrade
XGBoostSecond-order objective; γ,λ regularization; sparsity-aware default direction for missing values; weighted quantile sketch for approximate split finding; column blocks with cache-aware accessRobust, well-documented, the reference implementation
LightGBMLeaf-wise (best-first) growth; GOSS — keep large-gradient instances, subsample small-gradient ones with a compensating weight; EFB — bundle mutually exclusive sparse features into oneMuch faster on large n; leaf-wise growth overfits small data unless depth-limited
CatBoostOrdered target statistics for categorical encoding; ordered boosting — build each model on a prefix of a random permutation so residuals are computed by a model that never saw that pointRemoves the target-leakage bias that plain target encoding introduces; slower per tree

Why gradient boosting still leads on tabular data. Trees handle mixed types, monotone-invariant features, missingness, and irrelevant variables natively; the ensemble supplies smoothness; second-order boosting is a very efficient use of each split. Grinsztajn, Oyallon & Varoquaux attribute the gap to three things: neural nets are biased toward overly smooth solutions, are not rotation-invariant in a way that helps here, and are hurt by uninformative features — exactly the regime where trees' feature selection helps.

Ensemble Theory

Day 65 · 85 min
Bias–variance–covariance and ambiguity

For f=1M∑fm under squared loss: 𝔼(f−y)2=bias2+1Mvar+(1−1M)covar. Ambiguity decomposition (Krogh–Vedelsby): (f−y)2=1M∑m(fm−y)2−1M∑m(fm−f)2. The ensemble error equals the average error minus the diversity. Diversity is therefore free error reduction — but the two terms are not independent, since making members more diverse usually makes them individually worse.

Theorem — the super learner oracle inequality (van der Laan, Polley & Hubbard)

Let fˆSL be the cross-validated convex combination of K candidate learners. Then, up to a logK factor and under a bounded-loss condition, 𝔼[R(fˆSL)−R⋆]≤(1+ϵ)mink𝔼[R(fˆk)−R⋆]+O(logKn). The stacked ensemble performs asymptotically as well as the best single candidate — you cannot lose by adding a learner.

Stacking, done correctly. Build a level-one design from out-of-fold predictions; fit the meta-learner (usually non-negative least squares with a sum-to-one constraint) on those. Using in-sample predictions is fatal: the most overfit base learner has the smallest in-sample residuals and receives all the weight. This single implementation detail is the difference between the oracle inequality holding and not.

BMA vs stacking. Bayesian model averaging weights by posterior model probability and assumes the true model is in the set (ℳ-closed); as n→∞ it concentrates on one model. Stacking optimizes predictive performance and is appropriate in the ℳ-open world where no candidate is true; it typically spreads weight and predicts better. Yao et al.'s "stacking of predictive distributions" is the current recommendation for the Bayesian setting.

Limits. Ensembling cannot fix a shared bias — if all members share an assumption (e.g. additivity, or the same flawed features), averaging cannot escape it, and the covariance term dominates. Negative correlation learning explicitly penalizes ∑m(fm−f)2 during training to force diversity; it works but risks driving individual members below usefulness.

Interpretation with Theory

Day 65 · 85 min
Theorem — Shapley's axiomatic characterization

The unique attribution satisfying efficiency (∑jϕj=v(N)−v(∅)), symmetry, dummy, and additivity is ϕj=∑S⊆N∖{j}|S|!(|N|−|S|−1)!|N|![v(S∪{j})−v(S)].

The axioms are about the game v, not about the model. The entire interpretive content of SHAP is hidden in the choice of v(S) — how you "remove" the features outside S.

Definition of v(S)MeaningProblem
𝔼[f(X)∣XS=xS] (conditional)What we'd predict knowing only xSGives nonzero credit to features the model never uses, if they are correlated with used ones
𝔼[f(xS,XS)] (interventional / marginal)Break the dependence; evaluate off-manifoldEvaluates f where it was never trained; can be nonsense for strongly dependent features

These are genuinely different quantities and they disagree whenever features are dependent (Chen, Janizek, Lundberg & Lee; Sundararajan & Najmi). TreeSHAP computes exact Shapley values for tree ensembles in O(TLD2) using the tree structure — but its default path-dependent variant computes neither of the above cleanly; it uses the training-set path proportions, which is an approximation to the conditional expectation with its own biases.

The other tools, with their caveats

  • Partial dependence PDj(v)=𝔼X−j[f(v,X−j)] is a marginal-intervention curve on the model. It has a causal reading only if the model is causally correct and there is no confounding — which is essentially never. With correlated features it also averages over impossible combinations. ICE curves (one line per observation) reveal the heterogeneity that PD hides.
  • Permutation importance under correlation suffers the same off-manifold problem; conditional variants fix it at the cost of needing a conditional sampler.
  • Rashomon sets. There is typically a large set of models with near-equal accuracy that assign wildly different importances (Breiman's "Rashomon effect"; Fisher, Rudin & Dominici's model-class reliance; Semenova, Rudin & Parr). So "variable j is important" is under-determined by the data; the honest object is a variable importance cloud over the Rashomon set.
What interpretability methods can license

They can license claims about this fitted function: which inputs it responds to, where its decision boundary lies, whether it uses a protected attribute. They cannot license claims about the data-generating process, about what would happen under intervention, or about which variable "really matters" — those are causal questions requiring the machinery of Module 12. Presenting SHAP values as effect estimates is the most common misuse in applied ML, and it is a category error, not a technical imprecision.

Recitation 7: Trees and Ensembles Problem Set 🔴

Day 65 · 60 min · heavy day: 230 min total
  1. Nested subtrees. Prove that the cost-complexity minimizers are nested, and that the weakest-link rule produces exactly the breakpoint sequence. Where does the argument use that R(T) is additive over leaves?
  2. XGBoost gain. Derive wj⋆=−Gj/(Hj+λ) and the split gain from the second-order objective, then show that setting λ=0,γ=0 and hi≡1 recovers the ordinary variance-reduction criterion.
  3. Bagging a linear predictor. Show that for fˆ=Sy the bagged estimator has the same limit as the original, and quantify the small discrepancy from bootstrap resampling of rows.
  4. Misleading permutation importance. Construct X1≈X2 with only X1 used by the true function; show both get near-zero permutation importance in a model that splits on either (the "correlated features share credit" failure), and then repair it with conditional permutation.
  5. Implementation. Write gradient boosting with depth-2 trees from scratch; verify that with squared loss and ν=1 it reproduces forward stagewise least squares, and plot the training/test curves against ν.
Two papers

Foundational: Breiman (2001), Random forests. Modern: Chen & Guestrin (2016), XGBoost — §2 is the theory, §3–4 are the systems contribution; both matter.
