Kernel Methods & Gaussian Processes

Reading: LWK Ch. 1–7 · GPML Ch. 2–6 · Steinwart & Christmann · Berlinet & Thomas-Agnan. One object — a positive definite kernel — viewed as a feature map, a smoothness functional, and a prior covariance. The three views are the same theorem read three ways.

74

Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Spaces

Day 51 · 85 min
Definition

k:𝒳×𝒳→ℝ is positive definite if for all n, xi, c∈ℝn: ∑i,jcicjk(xi,xj)≥0. An RKHS ℋ is a Hilbert space of functions on 𝒳 in which evaluation f↦f(x) is a bounded linear functional; by Riesz there is kx∈ℋ with f(x)=⟨f,kx⟩ — the reproducing property.

Theorem — Moore–Aronszajn

Every positive definite k determines a unique RKHS with k(x,⋅)=kx, and conversely. Construction: take ℋ0=span{k(x,⋅)} with ⟨∑aikxi,∑bjkyj⟩=∑i,jaibjk(xi,yj) (well defined and PSD by definition), then complete.

So "choose a kernel" and "choose a Hilbert space of functions" are the same act. The feature map ϕ(x)=k(x,⋅) is canonical but not unique — k(x,y)=ϕ(x)⊤ϕ(y) can hold for many ϕ of different dimensions (e.g. the polynomial kernel has an explicit finite feature map and the canonical infinite one). What is unique is ℋ and its norm.

Theorem — Mercer

For continuous k on a compact 𝒳 with finite measure μ, the integral operator (Tkf)(x)=∫k(x,y)f(y)dμ(y) is compact, self-adjoint, positive, with eigenpairs (μj,ej) and k(x,y)=∑jμjej(x)ej(y)(absolutely,uniformlyconvergent),ℋ={f=∑ajej:‖f‖ℋ2=∑aj2μj<∞}.

The norm is a smoothness penalty. ‖f‖ℋ2=∑aj2/μj divides by the eigenvalues, so components along rapidly decaying eigenfunctions are expensive. For the Gaussian kernel μj decays geometrically and ℋ contains only analytic functions — extremely restrictive. For Matérn-ν, μj≍j−(2ν+d)/d and ℋ is norm-equivalent to the Sobolev space Wν+d/2,2. The eigenvalue decay is the whole story: it fixes the covering numbers (Lecture 32), hence the learning rate (Lecture 75), hence the sample complexity.

Where it breaks

The Gaussian RKHS does not contain the functions people assume: it contains no compactly supported nonzero function, and does not contain sin or polynomials. Statements like "the Gaussian kernel is universal so it can learn anything" conflate density in C(𝒳) (true — universality) with membership (false). The consequence is real: rates for KRR depend on the source condition f⋆∈range(Tkr), and if f⋆∉ℋ the rate degrades from n−2r2r+1/s to something slower.

The Representer Theorem and Kernel Ridge Regression

Day 52 · 85 min
Theorem — representer (Schölkopf, Herbrich & Smola)

Let Ω:[0,∞)→ℝ be strictly increasing and c an arbitrary loss. Any minimizer of minf∈ℋc((x1,y1,f(x1)),…,(xn,yn,f(xn)))+Ω(‖f‖ℋ) admits f=∑i=1nαik(xi,⋅).

proofDecompose f=f∥+f⊥ with f∥∈span{k(xi,⋅)}. By the reproducing property f(xi)=⟨f,kxi⟩=f∥(xi), so the loss is unchanged by dropping f⊥; but ‖f‖2=‖f∥‖2+‖f⊥‖2, so Ω strictly decreases. Hence f⊥=0 at any minimizer.

An infinite-dimensional variational problem collapses to n numbers. The semiparametric version allows an unpenalized finite-dimensional part f=∑αikxi+∑βjψj (used for the intercept in SVMs and for a linear trend in GPs). The generalized version replaces Ω(‖f‖) with any strictly monotone function of the norm.

Kernel ridge regression

With squared loss and Ω=λ‖f‖2: αˆ=(K+nλI)−1y, fˆ(x)=k(x)⊤(K+nλI)−1y. Equivalently fˆ= the posterior mean of a GP with covariance k and noise nλ. Effective degrees of freedom df(λ)=∑jμˆjμˆj+nλ using the kernel-matrix spectrum.

Learning rates under source and capacity conditions

Capacity: eigenvalues μj≍j−1/s, s∈(0,1] (smaller s = smaller space). Source: f⋆=Tkrg with ‖g‖<∞, r∈(0,1] (larger r = smoother truth). Then with λ≍n−12r+s, 𝔼‖fˆ−f⋆‖L22≍n−2r2r+s, which is minimax optimal over the corresponding class. For Sobolev-m in d dimensions this reproduces n−2m/(2m+d) — Lecture 59's rate, arrived at by an entirely different route.

Saturation. KRR's rate stops improving beyond r=1: extra smoothness of f⋆ buys nothing because the ridge penalty's bias saturates at O(λ). Spectral filters (iterated Tikhonov, Landweber/gradient-descent filtering, spectral cut-off) remove the saturation, which is the theoretical reason early-stopped gradient descent on the kernel objective can beat explicit ridge — implicit regularization with a different filter function.

Kernel Design

Day 53 · 85 min

Closure properties (the PD kernels form a closed convex cone): sums, positive scalar multiples, products (Schur), limits, k(x,y)=f(x)f(y), k(ψ(x),ψ(y)) for any ψ, and exp(k), hence exp(−‖x−y‖2/2σ2)=exp(−‖x‖2/2σ2)exp(x⊤y/σ2)exp(−‖y‖2/2σ2) is PD. Note that differences are not: k1−k2 need not be PD.

Theorem — Bochner

A continuous stationary k(x−y) is PD iff it is the Fourier transform of a finite nonnegative measure: k(τ)=∫eiω⊤τdS(ω). So designing a stationary kernel is designing a spectral density. The Gaussian kernel's spectral density is Gaussian (light tails ⇒ very smooth paths); Matérn's is a t-density (heavy tails ⇒ rougher paths).

Structured inputs. String kernels (count shared subsequences with gap penalties, computed by dynamic programming); graph kernels (random-walk, Weisfeiler–Lehman, shortest-path); set kernels; kernels on distributions via mean embeddings (next lecture). Each is PD by construction from the closure properties, usually as an inner product of explicit count features.

ARD uses k(x,y)=σ2exp(−∑d(xd−yd)2/2ℓd2) with per-dimension lengthscales; ℓd→∞ prunes dimension d. Learned by maximizing the marginal likelihood, this is a soft, differentiable form of feature selection — and, in the GP setting, the closest thing to an automatic escape from the curse of dimensionality (Lecture 59).

Marginal likelihood vs cross-validation

Marginal likelihood is cheaper (one fit, gradients available) and includes an automatic Occam factor, but it is a statement about the model class and is badly behaved under misspecification — a wrong kernel family can give a confidently wrong lengthscale. CV targets predictive performance directly and is robust to misspecification but is noisier and k-fold more expensive. Use marginal likelihood to explore, CV to decide.

Support Vector Machines I

Day 54 · 85 min

Hard margin primal: min12‖w‖2 s.t. yi(w⊤xi+b)≥1. The functional margin is yi(w⊤xi+b), the geometric margin is that over ‖w‖; fixing the functional margin at 1 is a normalization, and maximizing the geometric margin becomes minimizing ‖w‖.

Dual, derived

L=12‖w‖2−∑iαi[yi(w⊤xi+b)−1]. Stationarity gives w=∑iαiyixi and ∑iαiyi=0. Substituting: maxα∑iαi−12∑i,jαiαjyiyjk(xi,xj)s.t.α⪰0,∑iαiyi=0. Complementary slackness: αi>0⇒yif(xi)=1. Support vectors are exactly the points on the margin.

Soft margin. min12‖w‖2+C∑ξi s.t. yif(xi)≥1−ξi, ξ≥0. Eliminating ξi=(1−yif(xi))+ shows this is exactly hinge-loss regularized ERM with λ=1/(nC). The dual is unchanged except 0≤αi≤C — a box constraint. Now three categories: αi=0 (outside margin, irrelevant), 0<αi<C (on the margin, used to compute b), αi=C (inside the margin or misclassified).

Support Vector Machines II

Day 54 · 85 min
SMO — the analytic two-variable step

The constraint ∑αiyi=0 means no single α can move alone; the smallest feasible update is a pair. Fix all but α1,α2: then α1y1+α2y2=γ constant, so the QP is one-dimensional on a segment. Unclipped solution: α2new=α2+y2(E1−E2)η,η=k11+k22−2k12, with Ei=f(xi)−yi, then clip to [L,H] determined by the box and the line. Working-set selection by maximal KKT violation (or second-order gain) gives convergence.

SMO needs no QP solver and no matrix storage — the reason SVMs were practical in 1998. Its cost is O(n2) to O(n3) overall; combined with the fact that prediction costs O(|SV|) and |SV| grows linearly with n for noisy problems (Steinwart), SVMs do not scale to n≳105 without approximation (Lecture 83).

The family

  • ν-SVM. Reparameterize C by ν∈(0,1]: at the optimum ν is simultaneously an upper bound on the fraction of margin errors and a lower bound on the fraction of support vectors. Far more interpretable than C.
  • One-class SVM / support estimation. Separate the data from the origin in feature space with maximum margin; the solution estimates a level set of the density, giving a principled anomaly detector whose ν controls the expected outlier fraction.
  • SVR. ϵ-insensitive loss (|y−f(x)|−ϵ)+ gives sparsity in the regression setting: points inside the tube have α=0. This is the only common regression method with exactly sparse dual coefficients.
  • Structured output SVM. Margin over an exponentially large label set, with constraints w⊤[ψ(xi,yi)−ψ(xi,y)]≥Δ(yi,y)−ξi for all y. Cutting-plane training adds only the most violated constraint per round, needing loss-augmented inference argmaxy[w⊤ψ(x,y)+Δ(yi,y)] — the bridge to Lecture 107.

Leave-one-out bound. 𝔼[LOOerror]≤𝔼[|SV|]/n: removing a non-support vector does not change the solution. Cheap, but loose — it counts all support vectors as potential errors.

Kernelizing Classical Multivariate Analysis

Day 55 · 75 min

Centering in feature space. K˜=HKH with H=I−1n11⊤. Necessary before any kernel PCA/CCA; forgetting it is the most common implementation bug in this lecture.

Kernel PCA

Principal directions in feature space are v=∑iαiϕ(xi), and the eigenproblem K˜α=nλα with normalization α⊤K˜α=1. Projections: ⟨v,ϕ(x)⟩=∑iαik˜(xi,x). Costs O(n3) and yields up to n components even if the input dimension is 2.

The pre-image problem. A point in feature space generally has no pre-image in 𝒳 — the image of ϕ is a thin manifold, and linear combinations leave it. So kernel PCA denoising requires solving minz‖ϕ(z)−Pϕ(x)‖2, a nonconvex problem with no exact solution; fixed-point iterations for Gaussian kernels produce a weighted mean of the data. Treat kernel-PCA reconstructions as approximations with no guarantee.

Kernel CCA maximizes corr(⟨f,ϕ(x)⟩,⟨g,ψ(y)⟩). Without regularization the answer is degenerate — with n points and universal kernels you can always achieve correlation 1 — so a ridge term κ‖f‖2 is mandatory, not optional. Same story for kernel FDA.

Kernel k-means and spectral clustering. k-means in feature space uses only inner products; the objective equals a trace-maximization over cluster indicator matrices, and its spectral relaxation is exactly normalized-cut spectral clustering when the kernel is taken to be D−1W-related (Dhillon–Guan–Kulis). So kernel k-means and spectral clustering (Lecture 125) are the same algorithm under a kernel/affinity correspondence — a useful fact when you need a fast out-of-sample extension for a spectral method.

Kernel Mean Embeddings and Kernel Tests

Day 55 · 85 min
Mean embedding

μP=𝔼X∼P[k(X,⋅)]∈ℋ, well defined when 𝔼√k(X,X)<∞. It has the reproducing property ⟨μP,f⟩=𝔼Pf. k is characteristic if P↦μP is injective — true for Gaussian, Laplacian, and (by Bochner) any stationary kernel whose spectral measure has full support.

MMD

MMD(P,Q)=‖μP−μQ‖ℋ=sup‖f‖ℋ≤1(𝔼Pf−𝔼Qf), and MMD2=𝔼k(X,X′)+𝔼k(Y,Y′)−2𝔼k(X,Y). Unbiased estimator: 1n(n−1)∑i≠jk(xi,xj)+1m(m−1)∑i≠jk(yi,yj)−2nm∑i,jk(xi,yj) — a two-sample U-statistic. A linear-time version averages over disjoint pairs at a cost in power.

Null distribution. Under H0:P=Q the U-statistic is degenerate, so nMMD2ˆ⇒∑jλj(Zj2−1) — an infinite weighted χ2, not a normal. In practice calibrate by permutation (Lecture 22), which is exact under exchangeability and needs no eigenvalue estimates.

HSIC is MMD between PXY and PXPY with a product kernel; empirically HSIC=1n2tr(KHLH) — an independence test that detects any dependence with characteristic kernels, unlike correlation. Kernel conditional independence tests (KCI, HSCIC) extend this to X⊥Y∣Z and are the workhorse nonparametric CI test for constraint-based causal discovery (Lecture 143); their finite-sample calibration is the weak point, and Type I error inflation with continuous Z is well documented.

Kernel Stein discrepancy. Using the Stein operator 𝒜pf=f∇logp+∇f, KSD measures fit to an unnormalized model: KSD2=𝔼q[up(X,X′)] with up built from ∇logp only. This gives goodness-of-fit tests for models with intractable normalizers (Lecture 105) and underlies Stein variational methods.

Where it breaks

MMD's power depends critically on the bandwidth. The median heuristic is a default, not a theorem, and it fails badly when the difference between P and Q lives at a different scale from the data's overall spread. In high dimension MMD's power degrades — for fixed n, a Gaussian-kernel MMD test's power against a mean shift decays roughly as d−1/2 or worse; optimized-kernel and classifier-based tests are preferable there.

Gaussian Processes I: Regression

Day 56 · 85 min
Definition and posterior

f∼𝒢𝒫(m,k) means every finite marginal (f(x1),…,f(xn)) is Gaussian with mean m(xi) and covariance k(xi,xj); Kolmogorov's extension theorem guarantees such a process exists given consistent marginals. With y=f(X)+ε, ε∼N(0,σ2I): 𝔼[f⋆∣y]=k⋆⊤(K+σ2I)−1y,Cov[f⋆∣y]=k⋆⋆−k⋆⊤(K+σ2I)−1k⋆.

GP posterior — drag the kernel and see the prior become a posterior

The duality with KRR. The posterior mean is identical to kernel ridge regression with λ=σ2/n. What the GP adds is the second line: a posterior variance, which is available in closed form and — this is the striking part — does not depend on y. It is a purely geometric statement about how far x⋆ is from the design in the kernel metric. That is both its strength (you can plan experiments before collecting data — Lecture 116) and its weakness (it cannot detect that the model is wrong).

Marginal likelihood and the automatic Occam factor

logp(y∣X,θ)=−12y⊤Ky−1ydatafit−12logdetKycomplexity−n2log2π. Gradients: ∂∂θj=12tr[(αα⊤−Ky−1)∂Ky∂θj] with α=Ky−1y. The logdet term penalizes flexible kernels: a short lengthscale can fit anything, but spreads probability mass thinly, so the evidence falls. This is Occam's razor as a computable quantity (Lecture 111).

Where it breaks

The marginal likelihood is non-convex in the hyperparameters and routinely has multiple modes — typically one "long lengthscale, high noise" mode and one "short lengthscale, low noise" mode. These correspond to genuinely different explanations of the data; picking whichever the optimizer lands in, without restarts, is a real source of irreproducible GP results. Also: posterior variance under a misspecified kernel is confidently wrong, and no amount of data fixes it.

Gaussian Processes II: Beyond Gaussian Likelihoods

Day 57 · 85 min

With a non-Gaussian likelihood (Bernoulli, Poisson, Student-t) the posterior over f is no longer Gaussian and the marginal likelihood has no closed form. Four approximations, in increasing order of cost and fidelity:

MethodWhat it doesCharacter
LaplaceSecond-order Taylor of logp(f∣y) at the mode: q=N(fˆ,(K−1+W)−1), W=−∇2logp(y∣f)Cheapest; mode-centred, systematically underestimates the variance for skewed posteriors
EPIteratively moment-match each site ti(fi) against the tilted distributionUsually most accurate for GP classification; no convergence guarantee, can oscillate
Variational (KL)Maximize the ELBO over a Gaussian qProvides a lower bound on the evidence; mode-seeking, underestimates variance
MCMC (elliptical slice)Exact in the limit; exploits the Gaussian prior for a rejection-free slice samplerGold standard, O(n3) per sample

GP classification. With a probit likelihood, the Laplace predictive is p(y⋆=1)=Φ(f⋆√1+σ⋆2) — the variance shrinks the prediction toward 1/2, which is exactly the calibration behaviour Lecture 147 wants and which a plug-in point estimate lacks.

Multi-output GPs. The linear model of coregionalization writes Cov(fa(x),fb(x′))=Babk(x,x′) with B⪰0 shared across outputs — a Kronecker structure that also makes inference cheap. Learning B learns the task-relatedness matrix, giving a principled multi-task model.

Deep kernel learning uses kθ(gϕ(x),gϕ(x′)) with gϕ a neural network. Practically effective; theoretically it forfeits the marginal-likelihood Occam factor's protection, because ϕ has enough capacity to make the evidence uninformative, and overfitting of ϕ through the marginal likelihood is documented (Ober, Rasmussen & van der Wilk).

Posterior contraction

(van der Vaart & van Zanten) For a GP prior with RKHS matching the truth's smoothness β, the posterior contracts at the minimax rate n−β/(2β+d). If the prior is smoother than the truth, the rate degrades to the prior's smoothness — the prior cannot be un-learned. Placing a prior on the lengthscale (hierarchical) restores adaptivity across a range of β. This is the Bayesian mirror of Lepski's method (Lecture 59).

Scalable Kernel and GP Methods

Day 57 · 85 min

The bottleneck is (K+σ2I)−1 and logdet: O(n3) time, O(n2) memory. Four families of fix.

1. Low-rank approximations of K

Nyström: pick m landmarks, Kˆ=KnmKmm+Kmn. Error ‖K−Kˆ‖ is controlled by the tail eigenvalues; uniform landmark sampling suffices when the kernel-matrix leverage scores are flat, and ridge leverage score sampling with m=O˜(df(λ)) landmarks achieves the same statistical rate as exact KRR (Rudi–Camoriano–Rosasco). That is the key result: scaling need not cost accuracy.

Random Fourier features: by Bochner, k(x−y)=𝔼ω∼S[cos(ω⊤(x−y))], so z(x)=√2/D[cos(ωj⊤x+bj)]j=1D gives z(x)⊤z(y)≈k(x,y) with uniform error O(√dlog(σpdiam/ϵ)/D). Cost drops to O(nD2). Orthogonal random features (structured ω) reduce the variance; quadrature-based features can be far more efficient in low d.

2. Inducing points

SoR, DTC and FITC modify the prior so that f is conditionally independent given inducing values u at Z. FITC's diagonal correction is what makes it work — and also what makes it pathological: because it lets the noise level vary with x, FITC can drive the fitted noise to zero near inducing points, producing overconfident predictions and clustering inducing points on top of data.

Sparse variational GP (Titsias)

Do not modify the model; approximate the posterior. Maximize ℒ=logN(y∣0,Qnn+σ2I)−12σ2tr(Knn−Qnn),Qnn=KnmKmm−1Kmn. This is a genuine lower bound on the exact log marginal likelihood, so Z becomes a variational parameter that cannot overfit the way FITC's pseudo-inputs do. The trace term explicitly penalizes the approximation error — the mechanism that prevents variance collapse. Hensman's stochastic variant makes it minibatchable and pushes GPs to n>106.

3. Structure and 4. iterative solvers

SKI/KISS-GP interpolates onto a grid where K is Toeplitz/Kronecker, giving O(n) MVMs. Conjugate gradients + Lanczos avoid factorization entirely: CG solves Ky−1y in O(√κ) MVMs, stochastic Lanczos quadrature estimates logdet, and preconditioning (pivoted Cholesky) makes it robust. GPyTorch is built on this stack, and it is the modern default for exact GPs up to n≈105 on GPUs.

Bayesian Optimization

Day 58 · 85 min

Optimize f that is expensive, black-box, and possibly noisy, in as few evaluations as possible. Build a GP surrogate; choose the next point by maximizing an acquisition function that trades exploration against exploitation.

Theorem — GP-UCB regret (Srinivas et al.)

With βt=2log(|D|t2π2/6δ), cumulative regret satisfies RT=O*(√TγTβT) w.h.p., where γT=max|A|=T12log|I+σ−2KA| is the maximum information gain. Rates: γT=O(logd+1T) for the Gaussian kernel, O(Td(d+1)/(2ν+d(d+1))logT) for Matérn-ν. Hence RT/T→0: no-regret.

The proof is a direct extension of the UCB argument in Lecture 132, with γT replacing KlogT — the information gain plays the role of "effective number of arms". The submodularity of A↦γ(A) (Lecture 72) is what makes the greedy bound work.

Batch, constrained, high-dimensional. Batch BO uses local penalization, Thompson sampling, or the q-EI (computable by Monte Carlo with reparameterization). Constrained BO multiplies EI by the probability of feasibility. High-dimensional BO needs structure: additive decompositions, random embeddings (REMBO/ALEBO), or trust regions (TuRBO) — plain BO in d≳20 is unreliable.

When BO loses to random search

Bergstra & Bengio's point stands: if the objective has low effective dimensionality and a large evaluation budget is available in parallel, random search is competitive and trivially parallel. BO wins when evaluations are genuinely expensive (> minutes), the budget is small (< a few hundred), the dimension is modest, and the surrogate's smoothness assumption is roughly right. Reported BO wins on cheap objectives with hundreds of parallel workers should be read sceptically — and against the Hyperband/ASHA baseline, which exploits cheap low-fidelity evaluations that BO ignores.

Recitation 6: Kernel Methods Problem Set

Day 58 · 60 min
  1. Is it a kernel? Decide for each: min(x,y) on [0,∞) (yes — Brownian motion covariance); ‖x−y‖ (no); tanh(ax⊤y+b) (no, despite folklore); exp(−‖x−y‖1) (yes); 11+‖x−y‖2 (yes — rational quadratic). Prove each via Bochner or the closure properties.
  2. A new representer theorem. Prove it for Ω(‖f‖)=‖f‖p, p>0, and then find a regularizer for which it fails.
  3. SMO from scratch. Implement the two-variable analytic step with clipping and KKT-violation selection; verify against a reference SVM to 10−6 in the dual objective, and count support vectors as C varies.
  4. MMD by permutation. Implement the unbiased estimator and permutation calibration; measure power against a mean shift and a variance shift as d grows, and demonstrate the median-heuristic failure mode.
  5. GP hyperparameters. Optimize the marginal likelihood from 20 random restarts on a small dataset; plot the landscape in (lengthscale, noise) and locate the two modes.
Two papers

Foundational: Schölkopf, Herbrich & Smola (2001). Modern: Rahimi & Recht (2007) — and read Rahimi's 2017 NeurIPS test-of-time talk alongside it.
