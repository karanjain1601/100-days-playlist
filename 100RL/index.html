130 Days of Reinforcement Learning

From the agent–environment loop to RLHF, DPO and RL for reasoning, one lesson a day. Every lesson states its core idea, derives the maths instead of quoting it, walks through runnable code, says what breaks and why, and ends with practice sized to its time budget. Seven live demos run in the page, and eight milestone projects are built into the schedule.

Modules

Modules 0–7 follow Sutton & Barto almost chapter for chapter; Modules 8–14 extend into deep RL and current research. If you only want deep RL, start at Module 8 (Day 70) after skimming Modules 2, 5 and 7.

How every lesson is built

Each lesson follows the same arc as its video, scaled to its runtime. Here it is for a 40-minute lesson:

2′
intuition
formal treatment · maths on screen
line-by-line code
what breaks
ex.
  • 2 min recap of yesterday
  • 5 min intuition and the motivating problem
  • 15 min formal treatment with derivations
  • 12 min line-by-line code walkthrough
  • 4 min results, failure modes, what breaks and why
  • 2 min exercise and tomorrow's teaser

On the page that becomes: a one-paragraph core thesis, definition, theorem and derivation boxes, SVG diagrams, annotated Python (NumPy, Gymnasium ≥ 1.0, PyTorch), a What breaks box, and a Practice block sized to the lesson's hands-on budget. Two schedules work: five lessons a week for 26 weeks, or one a day for 130 days.

    Core references

    Primary textSutton & Barto, Reinforcement Learning: An Introduction, 2nd edition (2018), freely available from the authors. Modules 0–7 map onto it almost chapter for chapter.
    Lecture coursesDavid Silver's UCL course · Berkeley CS285 Deep RL (Sergey Levine) · Stanford CS234 · the DeepMind × UCL RL lecture series · OpenAI Spinning Up in Deep RL.
    CodeCleanRL (single-file implementations, ideal for study) · Stable-Baselines3 · Tianshou · the authors' original repositories for TD3, SAC and Rainbow.
    PapersEach lesson's Read line lists the canonical papers for it. RL is a field where the original papers are unusually readable.
