Adam = momentum + RMSProp + a correction

\[ \begin{aligned} \mathbf m_t &= \beta_1\mathbf m_{t-1}+(1-\beta_1)\mathbf g_t && \text{first moment (momentum)}\\ \mathbf v_t &= \beta_2\mathbf v_{t-1}+(1-\beta_2)\mathbf g_t^2 && \text{second moment (RMSProp)}\\ \hat{\mathbf m}_t &= \frac{\mathbf m_t}{1-\beta_1^{t}},\qquad \hat{\mathbf v}_t = \frac{\mathbf v_t}{1-\beta_2^{t}} && \text{bias correction}\\ \theta_{t+1} &= \theta_t-\eta\,\frac{\hat{\mathbf m}_t}{\sqrt{\hat{\mathbf v}_t}+\varepsilon} \end{aligned} \]
Why bias correction is needed

\(\mathbf m_0=\mathbf 0\). Unrolling the EMA with a constant gradient \(g\):

\[ m_t=(1-\beta_1)\sum_{i=1}^{t}\beta_1^{t-i}g = g\left(1-\beta_1^{t}\right) \]

At \(t=1\) with \(\beta_1=0.9\), \(m_1=0.1g\) — a tenfold underestimate. Worse for \(v\): with \(\beta_2=0.999\), \(v_1=0.001g^2\), so \(\sqrt{v_1}\approx0.032\lvert g\rvert\) and the raw step would be roughly \(31\eta\). Dividing by \((1-\beta^t)\) removes the bias exactly, and the factor decays to 1 as \(t\) grows. Without it, Adam's first few hundred steps are wildly oversized — which is also why warmup helps even when bias correction is present.

AdamW — the fix everyone needs

Why weight_decay in Adam is wrong

L2 regularisation adds \(\lambda\theta\) to the gradient. In Adam that term then passes through the adaptive denominator:

\[ \theta \mathrel{-}= \eta\frac{\hat m_t(\mathbf g+\lambda\theta)}{\sqrt{\hat v_t}+\varepsilon} \]

So parameters with large historical gradients get a small denominator-scaled decay, and rarely-updated parameters get a large one. The regularisation strength becomes an accident of gradient history — the opposite of what you want. AdamW decouples it:

\[ \theta_{t+1}=\theta_t-\eta\left(\frac{\hat{\mathbf m}_t}{\sqrt{\hat{\mathbf v}_t}+\varepsilon}+\lambda\theta_t\right) \]

Use AdamW. Always. It is the default for every transformer trained since 2018. And exclude biases and normalisation parameters from decay — shrinking a LayerNorm gain towards zero is actively harmful.

decay, no_decay = [], []
for name, p in model.named_parameters():
    if not p.requires_grad: continue
    if p.ndim < 2 or "bias" in name or "norm" in name.lower():
        no_decay.append(p)          # biases, LayerNorm/BatchNorm gains, embeddings-ish
    else:
        decay.append(p)             # weight matrices only

opt = torch.optim.AdamW([
    {"params": decay,    "weight_decay": 0.1},
    {"params": no_decay, "weight_decay": 0.0},
], lr=3e-4, betas=(0.9, 0.95), eps=1e-8)

The Adam-vs-SGD debate, settled as far as it can be

Well-tuned SGD with momentum still edges out Adam on ImageNet-style CNN classification, by a few tenths of a percent. Adam wins essentially everywhere else — transformers, GANs, RL, anything with sparse gradients or wildly different parameter scales — and it wins enormously on tuning effort, because its usable LR range is much wider. Practically: AdamW by default; SGD+momentum if you are chasing a CNN leaderboard number and have budget to tune.

What to actually do

Use AdamW with \(\eta=3\times10^{-4}\), \(\beta=(0.9,0.95)\), decay 0.1 on weight matrices only, plus warmup and cosine decay (Day 29). That configuration trains essentially everything in Phases 4–7. Change it only when you have a measured reason.
