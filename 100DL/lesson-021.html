Regression losses and what their gradients do

LossFormulaGradient w.r.t. \(\hat y\)Behaviour
MSE\((y-\hat y)^2\)\(2(\hat y-y)\)Grows with error → outliers dominate. Predicts the mean.
MAE\(\lvert y-\hat y\rvert\)\(\operatorname{sign}(\hat y-y)\)Constant magnitude → robust, but never anneals. Predicts the median.
Huberquadratic inside \(\delta\), linear outsideclipped at \(\delta\)Both behaviours. The sensible default for noisy regression.

"Predicts the mean vs the median" is not trivia — it is a modelling decision. If your targets have a heavy tail (house prices, waiting times), MSE will chase the tail and MAE will not.

\[ L_\delta(y,\hat y)=\begin{cases}\tfrac12(y-\hat y)^2 & \lvert y-\hat y\rvert\le\delta\\ \delta\left(\lvert y-\hat y\rvert-\tfrac12\delta\right)&\text{otherwise}\end{cases} \]

Focal loss — for when 99% of your examples are background

In object detection, most anchor boxes are negatives. Even with individually tiny losses, they swamp the gradient by sheer count. Focal loss down-weights easy examples by a factor that shrinks as confidence grows:

\[ \mathrm{FL}(p_t)=-\alpha_t\,(1-p_t)^{\gamma}\log(p_t) \]

With \(\gamma=2\), an example predicted at \(p_t=0.9\) has its loss scaled by \((0.1)^2=0.01\) — a hundredfold reduction — while a hard example at \(p_t=0.1\) is scaled by \(0.81\), essentially untouched. Setting \(\gamma=0\) recovers plain weighted cross-entropy. This is the loss that made one-stage detectors competitive (Day 52).

The logits-vs-probabilities trap, stated once more

Pitfall

In PyTorch, CrossEntropyLoss and BCEWithLogitsLoss take logits. NLLLoss takes log-probabilities. BCELoss takes probabilities. Mixing these up is silent: the model still trains, just badly. Write the expected input as a comment above every loss you construct.

Other losses you will meet, and where

  • Hinge \(\max(0,1-y\hat y)\) — SVMs. Zero loss once the margin is satisfied. Rare in DL but the margin idea returns in ArcFace (Day 54-b).
  • KL divergence — matching one distribution to another: knowledge distillation (Day 97), VAEs (Day 89), the RLHF policy penalty (Day 85).
  • Contrastive / triplet / InfoNCE — learning an embedding space where similar things are close. Day 54-b, and the foundation of CLIP and SimCLR.
  • Dice / IoU / Tversky — segmentation, where the foreground is a few percent of the pixels and pixel-wise CE is misleading (Day 53).
  • CTC — sequence prediction without alignment: speech and OCR (Days 60, 94).

Label smoothing, since it belongs here

Replace the one-hot target with \(y_k^{\text{LS}}=(1-\varepsilon)y_k+\varepsilon/K\). The model is now asked to be confident but not certain, which stops logits growing without bound, improves calibration, and usually adds a fraction of a percent on ImageNet-scale problems. \(\varepsilon=0.1\) is standard. Full treatment on Day 31.

import torch, torch.nn as nn, torch.nn.functional as F

class FocalLoss(nn.Module):
    """Binary focal loss. Input = raw logits."""
    def __init__(self, alpha=0.25, gamma=2.0):
        super().__init__()
        self.alpha, self.gamma = alpha, gamma

    def forward(self, logits, targets):
        # never call sigmoid then log -- use the fused, stable version
        bce = F.binary_cross_entropy_with_logits(logits, targets, reduction="none")
        p   = torch.sigmoid(logits)
        p_t = p * targets + (1 - p) * (1 - targets)
        a_t = self.alpha * targets + (1 - self.alpha) * (1 - targets)
        return (a_t * (1 - p_t) ** self.gamma * bce).mean()
Exercise

Plot MSE, MAE and Huber (\(\delta=1\)) and their gradients on the same axes over \([-5,5]\). Then build a regression dataset, inject 5% outliers at 10× scale, and fit with each. Compare the learned slopes. MSE will be visibly dragged; that is the entire argument for robust losses in one plot.
