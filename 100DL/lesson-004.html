Rank is the number of independent directions that survive

\(\operatorname{rank}(W)\) is the dimension of the span of \(W\)'s columns — the dimension of the output space the layer can actually reach. A \(1000\times1000\) matrix of rank 5 has a million numbers but only carries five directions' worth of information. This is not a curiosity: measured weight-update matrices in fine-tuning turn out to be close to low rank, which is the entire premise of LoRA (Day 84-b).

Eigenvectors: the directions a matrix does not rotate

\(A\mathbf v=\lambda\mathbf v\). Applying \(A\) to \(\mathbf v\) only stretches it. Applying \(A\) repeatedly, \(A^k\mathbf v=\lambda^k\mathbf v\), so the largest \(\lvert\lambda\rvert\) dominates. That is exactly the mechanism behind exploding and vanishing gradients through a deep stack (Day 26): repeated multiplication by a Jacobian whose spectral radius is \(>1\) or \(\lt 1\).

If \(A\) has \(n\) independent eigenvectors, \(A=Q\Lambda Q^{-1}\), and \(A^k=Q\Lambda^k Q^{-1}\) — powers become trivial. For symmetric \(A\), \(Q\) is orthogonal and \(A=Q\Lambda Q\T\), the spectral theorem.

SVD: every matrix, no conditions attached

Eigendecomposition needs a square, diagonalisable matrix. SVD needs nothing:

\[ A = U\Sigma V\T,\qquad A\in\R^{m\times n},\; U\in\R^{m\times m},\;\Sigma\in\R^{m\times n},\;V\in\R^{n\times n} \]

with \(U,V\) orthogonal and \(\Sigma\) diagonal with \(\sigma_1\ge\sigma_2\ge\dots\ge0\). The geometric reading: every linear map is a rotation, then an axis-aligned scaling, then another rotation. That is all a matrix can ever do.

Low-rank approximation, and why it matters more than it looks

Truncate to the top \(k\) singular values: \(A_k=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sigma_i\mathbf u_i\mathbf v_i\T\). The Eckart–Young theorem says this is the best possible rank-\(k\) approximation in both Frobenius and spectral norm. You store \(k(m+n+1)\) numbers instead of \(mn\).

PCA falls out of SVD

Centre your data matrix \(X\in\R^{N\times d}\) so each column has mean zero. The covariance is \(C=\frac{1}{N-1}X\T X\). Take the SVD \(X=U\Sigma V\T\); then

\[ X\T X = V\Sigma\T U\T U\Sigma V\T = V\Sigma^2 V\T \]

so the columns of \(V\) are the eigenvectors of the covariance (the principal directions) and the eigenvalues are \(\sigma_i^2/(N-1)\) (the variance along each). Projecting is \(Z=XV_k\). You never need to form \(C\) — doing SVD directly on \(X\) is both faster and numerically better, because squaring the data squares the condition number.

Sanity check

Run PCA on MNIST. About 150 of the 784 components hold ~95% of the variance, and the top components look like blurry stroke templates. Keep this number in mind: real data lives on a much lower-dimensional manifold than its ambient dimension, which is why learning is possible at all.

Exercise

Implement rank-\(k\) image compression with np.linalg.svd. Plot reconstruction error and storage cost against \(k\) on the same axes and find the knee. Then plot the singular value spectrum on a log scale — the shape of that curve is the same diagnostic you will use to choose a LoRA rank on Day 84-b.
